# Practice plan — the hardest textbook exercises (Listas 1–7)

30 exercises from the course books, chosen as the hardest available on the topics of Listas 1–7.
**References only** — open the book at the page given; problem statements are not reproduced.
Solutions: the 16 Kurlat exercises are solved step by step (with verification code) in
`Resolucao/kurlat_solutions_*.pdf`; the 14 Romer problems are solved in
[`Listas/hardest-solutions.pdf`](hardest-solutions.pdf).

- **Sources:** Kurlat (2020) — every exercise marked *advanced* in [[exercises-index]]; Romer (2012),
  *Advanced Macroeconomics*, 4th ed. — end-of-chapter problems on the course's topics, numbers and
  pages verified in the converted book (`Livros/`).
- **Levels:** *Advanced* = Kurlat's hardest (cross topics, non-trivial derivation); *Graduate* =
  Romer (first-year PhD problem sets). There is no foundations or exam-level material here.
- **Left out on purpose (course scope):** Romer's Diamond overlapping-generations problems, the
  stochastic consumption and asset-pricing problems (8.3–8.6, 8.8–8.11, 8.14), the reservation-wage
  recursions (10.10–10.11, dynamic programming), rational-expectations DSGE problems (6.8–6.9, ch. 7)
  and Kurlat ch. 8 and 12–15. Kurlat exercises the professor already assigned in Listas 1–3 (1.2,
  2.2, 3.3, 4.1, 4.3, 6.1, 6.3, 6.5, 6.6) are excluded too.

## Suggested study order

Follow the course: Measurement → Growth → Consumption → Labor → General equilibrium → Money →
AS–AD. Inside each block do the Kurlat exercise first (it fixes the model in the course's
notation), then the Romer problem (the same mechanism in general form, with proofs). Budget about
45–90 minutes per exercise; attempt it fully before opening the solution.
