Taxes, Timing and the Limit

Lump-sum taxes enter the household's problem only through their present value, so moving them across periods changes nothing — until a borrowing limit binds. A tax on the return to saving is different in kind: it does not just make the household poorer, it changes the price of future consumption.

Where marks were lost (graded Lista 3)
  • 1(a), 1(d) — c₁ and c₂ solved, savings a never solved for. It is asked for: a = a₀ + y₁ − τ₁ − c₁.
  • 1(c) — the sign condition was right and the conclusion reversed. Correct: σ > 1 ⟹ c₁ rises with r; σ < 1 ⟹ it falls.
  • 1(e) — proved taxes enter only through τ₁ + τ₂⁄(1+r), then wrote “yes, timing matters”. The proof says the opposite: without a binding limit, timing does not matter.
  • 2(b) — one example of a binding limit where two were asked (e.g. a steep income profile, the young professional with low y₁ and high y₂; a transitory fall in y₁; also impatience, low β, or taxes front-loaded in τ₁).
  • 2(d) — derived (c₂⁄c₁)σ = β(1+r−τs) but never compared it with the lump sum or named the distorted margin: the intertemporal one.

Taxes and the limit
Lump sum: c₂ = y₂ − τ₂ + (1+r)a. Savings tax: c₂ = y₂ + (1+r−τs)a, and τ₂ is off.
a ≥ −b. b = 0 forbids borrowing; the right end removes the limit.
Why only the present value matters

Substitute a from period 1 into period 2: c₁ + c₂⁄(1+r) = a₀ + y₁ + y₂⁄(1+r) − [τ₁ + τ₂⁄(1+r)]. Taxes appear only through the bracket, and the Euler equation c₂⁄c₁ = [β(1+r)]1/σ does not contain them at all. A swap that keeps the bracket fixed leaves the budget set and hence (c₁, c₂) unchanged; the household saves the cut to pay the later bill. The limit a ≥ −b is the one place τ₁ enters on its own: binding, c₁ = a₀ + y₁ − τ₁ + b. Derivation in 05-ricardian-and-constraints.md §5.1–§5.3.
