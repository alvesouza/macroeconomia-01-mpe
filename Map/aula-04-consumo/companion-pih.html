Wealth, Not Income

The same household, hit with the same number of dollars. If the shock is a one-off it is almost entirely saved; if it is permanent it is almost entirely spent. That factor-of-twenty difference is the permanent income hypothesis — and it disappears the moment the household cannot borrow.

The shock
0 = a one-off windfall this period. 1 = a permanent raise, every period from now on. In between: a shock that decays.
The household
The remaining horizon. A windfall is spread over it, so the MPC falls roughly as 1⁄T — but only when r is near zero; discounting raises it above that.
Turn this on and the household cannot borrow against future income. It consumes what it receives, and the Keynesian consumption function becomes exactly right for it.
The annuity formula

With r = ρ the optimal path is flat at c = (r⁄(1+r))·W⁄[1 − (1+r)−T], so a windfall of 1 raises consumption by exactly that annuity factor. As T → ∞ it becomes r⁄(1+r) ≈ 0.038 — consume the interest, never the principal. A permanent rise of 1 per period raises W by (1+r)⁄r, so consumption rises by exactly 1: an MPC of one. The ratio between the two is 26 at r = 4%. Derivation in 04-permanent-income.md §4.3.
