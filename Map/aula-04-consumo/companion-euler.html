Two Periods, One Line

The budget line always passes through the endowment, so a change in the interest rate pivots it about that point. Everything about income and substitution effects follows from that one geometric fact — including why a higher interest rate can make a saver save less.

Endowment
Front-load income and the household lends; back-load it and the household borrows. The sign of the income effect flips with it.
Preferences and prices
β = 1⁄(1+ρ). When β(1+r) = 1 the path is exactly flat, whatever σ is.
σ is risk aversion; 1⁄σ is the elasticity of intertemporal substitution. σ = 1 is the knife-edge where the two effects cancel on the allocation.
Reading the compensated line

The dashed line is the budget line at the new interest rate, shifted until the household could just afford its old bundle. Moving from the old optimum to the tangency on that dashed line is the pure substitution effect; moving from there to the new optimum is the income effect. Its direction is set by the household's net trade — which is why lenders and borrowers differ. Derivation in 03-income-and-substitution.md §3.2.
