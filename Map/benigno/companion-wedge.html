The Wedge

The planner hires labour until the marginal rate of substitution reaches labour's product, a. The market stops earlier, where it reaches the wage firms pay, a − μ. The whole distance between natural and efficient output is that mark-up wedge. Productivity moves both targets; the mark-up moves only one.

MRS: log(v′/u′) = η(y − a) + σ⁻¹(y − g) MRT = a (planner) a − μ (wage firms pay) lost surplus

The wedge

Monopoly power and taxes together, eq. (13). Lowers the wage firms pay below labour's product.

Technology and resources (efficient shocks)

Raises labour's product and shifts MRS: yₙ and yₑ rise together.

Less consumption at each output raises marginal utility: households work more, and yₙ and yₑ rise together.

Preferences, Benigno's calibration

Steeper MRS: the same wedge costs fewer units of output.

Lower σ makes marginal utility fall faster with consumption, steepening MRS.

Not exposed: α and κ. Price stickiness appears in neither yₙ nor yₑ, so a control for it would change nothing on this page. That absence is the point: the wedge is market power, not rigidity. Levels are log-deviations from an efficient steady state (μ = 0 there), in per cent. The lost-surplus figure is the triangle ½(σ⁻¹+η)(yₙ − yₑ)², the second-order welfare cost in units of steady-state output.

Model. Household: log MRS = η(y − a) + σ⁻¹(y − g), from v(L) = L^{1+η}/(1+η), u′(C) = C^{−σ̃⁻¹} and y = s_c c + g. Planner: MRS = a (eq. 19). Market: MRS = a − μ (eq. 14 → 15). Result: yₙ − yₑ = −μ/(σ⁻¹+η), Benigno §4.4, p. 509.
