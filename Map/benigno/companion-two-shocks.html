Two Shocks

Two changes, two diagrams. A temporary rise in firms' desired mark-up moves AS and leaves AD alone. A cut in the nominal rate moves AD and leaves AS alone. The first gives stagflation; the second, a boom. In the first case the two output gaps have opposite signs.

(a) Desired mark-up rises

AS moves up and left through (pᵉ, yₙ′). AD does not move: the shock is temporary, so ȳₙ is unchanged.

Held fixed: ρ = 2%, p̄ = pᵉ = 0, g = ḡ, no tax changes. Initial rate i = rₙ, so both panels start at E = (0, 0) with yₙ = yₑ = 0. All quantities are log-deviations from an efficient steady state, in per cent.

Model. AS: p − pᵉ = κ(y − yₙ), eq. (17). AD: y = ȳₙ − σ[i − (p̄ − p) − ρ], eq. (21). yₙ = −μ/(σ⁻¹+η) from eq. (15); yₑ unaffected by μ, eq. (19). Closed form (note 04): y − yₙ = σ(rₙ − i)/(1+σκ), rₙ = ρ + σ⁻¹(ȳₙ − yₙ).
