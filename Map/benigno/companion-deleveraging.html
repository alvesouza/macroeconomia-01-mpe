When Demand Slopes Up

Put a borrowing limit on some households and the AD curve changes slope. Prices now move the real value of nominal debt — the Fisher effect — and with enough debt the demand curve turns upward, falling prices become contractionary, and the spending multiplier goes past one.

Who owes what

Borrowers are 1 − χ. Only savers respond to the interest rate, so a low χ weakens conventional monetary policy.

The stock the Fisher effect works on. Benigno uses 1.2, i.e. 120% of GDP.

Enters the Fisher term through 1 − β: patient agents, weak effect.

Change in the debt limit, ratio to GDP. Negative is deleveraging: AD shifts left.

Structure

Without debt this alone would set the AD slope, at −1/σ.

More flexible prices — lower α, steeper AS — deepen the contraction here. That is the paradox of flexibility.
