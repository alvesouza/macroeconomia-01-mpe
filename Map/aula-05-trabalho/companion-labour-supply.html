The Backward Bend

The wage is the price of leisure and a source of income, so a wage rise pulls hours in both directions. Where the two balance is a matter of preferences — and the same curve carries three different elasticities depending on what you hold fixed.

Preferences and income
u = ln c + θ ln ℓ. With π = 0 the income and substitution effects cancel exactly and hours do not depend on the wage at all — the balanced-growth case.
Non-wage income: a partner's earnings, a transfer, an inheritance. Raising it is a pure income effect — the clean test of the model.
The Frisch elasticity
η is the inverse Frisch elasticity — the same η that sets the slope of the AS curve in session 8. Micro studies of prime-age men suggest η between 3 and 10; Prescott's calculation needs η near 1.
Effective marginal tax wedge in Europe against the US. Prescott's numbers are 0.60 and 0.40.
Why εF ≥ εH ≥ εM

Each holds something looser fixed than the last, so each allows more adjustment. The Slutsky decomposition is εM = εH + (wh⁄c)·επ, and since leisure is a normal good επ < 0, so the Marshallian is the smallest.
