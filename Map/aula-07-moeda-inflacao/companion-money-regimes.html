Who Moves When Money Moves

MV = PY cannot fail — velocity is whatever number makes it hold — so it says nothing about who moves. The money market M = p·mD(Y, i) is one equation, and it can determine exactly one unknown. Which one is a choice made by two switches: what the central bank holds fixed, and whether prices can move.

Where marks were lost

  • 1(a), half credit. The identity was stated, but not that V ≡ PY/M is a residual — so the equation cannot fail, and causation needs an added behavioural assumption (stable V, real-side Y, exogenous M).
  • 1(b), half credit. V = √(2iY/F) was derived and left there. The mark is the reading: velocity rises with i and with Y, so it is not a constant.
  • 1(c). “No clear mechanism” — true, but the item then needs the split (Y↑ and/or i↓, one equation in two unknowns) and the flexible-price answer, which was never given: p rises one-for-one, money is neutral.
  • 1(d). Missing: under an interest-rate target the money market determines M, not i. Supply is horizontal and money is endogenous.
  • 2(c). π = μ − ½(g+f) was derived but read as “cheaper money raises inflation”. The reading is: a falling F lowers money demand, so the same μ chases smaller desired real balances and prices must rise faster — velocity rises.

Why Benigno has no LM curve

Under a rate target the supply of money is horizontal at ī. The money market no longer determines i — the bank announced it — it determines M, which the bank then supplies passively. A relation that only ever tells you a quantity nobody uses is dropped, and the model runs on the Euler equation (AD) and price setting (AS) alone. See Map/aula-08-adas-micro/00-index.md.

Log-differentiate mD = √(FY/2i): d ln m = ½ d ln Y − ½ d ln i. Every result on this page is that line solved for a different unknown.
