The Elasticity Dial

Inflation is not money growth. It is money growth minus the growth of the demand for real balances — and that second term is an elasticity times a growth rate. Move the dial from one half to one and a central bank that never changed its rule misses its target by one and a half points a year, every year.

The policy rule
The growth rate of the money stock the central bank commits to. Under a constant-money-growth rule this is the only instrument.
What the bank is aiming at. It moves the reference line, not the economy.
The economy
Trend growth of real output. It is the only reason a growing economy wants to hold more money at an unchanged interest rate.
How much real money demand rises with income. Baumol–Tobin says one half — there are scale economies in cash management. The Cambridge equation says one, which is the same thing as a trendless velocity.
The rate at which the cost of one trip to the bank is falling. ATMs, debit cards and Pix all make it negative, which shrinks money demand with no change in income or interest rates.
Where the expression comes from

Money market equilibrium holds at every date: MS = p·mD(Y, i). Take logs, differentiate in time, and note that a constant nominal rate kills the interest term — not because money demand is interest-inelastic, but because i is not moving. What is left is μ = π + η·g. Let the cost of a trip drift too, and Baumol–Tobin's mD = √(YF/2i) adds ½·f, giving π = μ − ½(g+f). This page writes the general form π = μ − η(g+f): the elasticity of money demand with respect to F is tied to η, which is exact in Baumol–Tobin, where both are ½.

The trap the algebra hides: η multiplies g, never μ. With g = 0 every extra unit of money lands on prices, whatever the elasticity is — set g and f to zero and watch π sit on μ.

Defaults are Problem Set 6's own calibration: μ = 3.5%, g = 3%, π* = 2%, η = ½ (Kurlat §10.4, p. 199–202), f = 0. The Baumol–Tobin read-outs are evaluated at the reference household of companion-baumol-tobin.html: Y = 1000, i = 5%, F = 2.
