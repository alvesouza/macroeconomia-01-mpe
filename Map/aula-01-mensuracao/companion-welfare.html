Flow value of being alive at zero consumption. It has no market price, and the life-expectancy term is entirely at its mercy.
Where the −s²⁄2 comes from

If consumption is lognormal, E[ln c] = ln E[c] − s²⁄2. Expected utility runs on the mean of the log, while GDP per head reports the log of the mean. The wedge between them is Jensen's inequality, and it is exactly the inequality penalty. With general σ the penalty is σs²⁄2 — linear in risk aversion, which is why σ is the parameter that decides how much inequality costs. Derivation in 05-beyond-gdp.md §5.4.
