Reading a Log Scale

The same two economies, drawn twice. On the left, levels: the rich country pulls away and the poor one looks flat. On the right, logs: the slope is the growth rate, parallel lines mean a constant income ratio, and convergence is visible as lines that meet. Most misreadings of a growth chart are a confusion between these two panels.

Why the slope is the growth rate

d(ln Y)/dt = Ẏ⁄Y. So a straight line on the log panel is constant proportional growth, and the vertical distance between two lines is ln(YA⁄YB) — a constant distance is a constant ratio, which is the absence of convergence however small the gap looks. Derivation in 03-growth-arithmetic.md §3.5.
