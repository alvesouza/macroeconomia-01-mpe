Two Countries, Two Baskets

Why is a haircut cheap in a poor country? Not because the country is poor. Because wages are set by productivity in tradables and paid to barbers whose productivity has not moved. That one line — PN/PT = AT/AN — is the whole of Balassa–Samuelson, and it is what makes the PPP correction large and predictable rather than noise.

Tradables: manufacturing, modern agriculture. This is where poor countries are furthest behind.
Non-tradables: haircuts, bus rides, domestic service. The gap here is much smaller — which is the entire mechanism.
Set both to the same value and the price-level gap vanishes however poor the country is. Poverty predicts nothing; asymmetric productivity does.

The three lines of algebra

Law of one price in tradables sets PT = 1 everywhere. Labour mobility equalises wages across sectors: W = PTAT = PNAN. Divide, and PN/PT = AT/AN. With a Cobb–Douglas basket the price level is P = (AT/AN)γ. Full derivation in 04-cross-country-and-ppp.md §4.3.
