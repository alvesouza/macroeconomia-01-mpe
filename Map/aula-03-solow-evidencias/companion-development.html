Whose Fault Is the Gap?

A country is a tenth as rich as the United States. How much of that is missing capital, how much is missing schooling, and how much is the residual we call productivity? The answer depends on a modelling choice most readers never notice — and the same parameters produce the interest rate that should prevail there, which is where the capital hypothesis dies.

The exponent on h changes with the method

K/L form: y = A kα h1−α, so schooling enters as h1−α. K/Y form: y = A1/(1−α) (K/Y)α/(1−α) h, so it enters as h1. Using the first exponent in the second decomposition understates human capital by about a third. Derivation in 05-growth-accounting-and-tfp.md §5.3–§5.4.
