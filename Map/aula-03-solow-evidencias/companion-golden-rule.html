The Golden Rule

Steady-state consumption is a hump in the saving rate, peaking at s = α. To the right of the peak the economy is dynamically inefficient and cutting saving makes every generation better off at once. To the left it is not inefficient at all — only impatient, and this model has nothing to say about that.

The observable test

You never need to measure the capital stock. Dynamic inefficiency is f′(k) < δ+n+g, which multiplied by k is capital income < investment — two national-accounts line items. In US data capital income is around 35% of GDP and investment around 20%, so the US is comfortably below the Golden Rule. Every developed economy that has been checked is.
