Two Economies, One Border

North and South are two closed Solow economies with different technologies, saving rates and population growth. Each converges to its own steady state. Then the border opens: capital and labour are pooled and everyone produces with the South's technology. Per-worker output moves in opposite directions on each side, while total output rises.

Why the gain has two sources

Split the change in total output by who produces it: YU − (YN+YS) = LN(yU−yN) + LS(yU−yS). The North term is large and positive: its workers get a better technology (AS, αS) and more capital each. The South term is negative: the South's capital is now spread over the North's workers too. The sum is positive at the list's values because a unit of capital moved North earns far more there — its marginal product at kN,0 on the South's technology is several times the South's own.

Where marks were lost. 1(b) called the two paths "divergence" — wrong: each economy converges to its own steady state (conditional convergence); the gap in levels persists only because the parameters differ. 1(c) gave the changes in y but not where each comes from (South: capital dilution; North: better technology plus more capital per worker). 1(d) gave k*U, y*U without explaining why y*U < y*S: nU > nS, and faster labour growth dilutes capital.
