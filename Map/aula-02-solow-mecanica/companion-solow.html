The Solow Diagram

One concave curve, one straight line, one crossing. Everything in this lecture is a statement about where they meet and how fast the economy gets there. The dotted path is the exact discrete law; the solid one is the continuous approximation everybody memorises — the gap between them is the factor 1/(1+n).

Why they cross exactly once

s f(k)/k is strictly decreasing — the chord from the origin lies above the tangent when f is concave — and it runs from +∞ (first Inada condition) down to 0 (second Inada condition). A strictly decreasing function crossing a positive constant does so exactly once. Diminishing returns give at most one crossing; Inada gives at least one.
