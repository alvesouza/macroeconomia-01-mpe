Level, Not Rate

Raise the saving rate permanently. Growth in output per worker jumps, then dies away; the level stays permanently higher. The shaded area under the growth spike is exactly the permanent log level gain — which is the precise sense in which a level effect is not a rate effect.

The policy
Structure
α governs both the size of the level gain, through α⁄(1−α), and the speed, through λ = (1−α)(δ+n). A larger α means a bigger prize collected more slowly.
The integral identity

∫ (ẏ⁄y) dt from the date of the change to infinity equals ln yss(s₁) − ln yss(s₀). A finite integral is a level effect. A permanent change in the growth rate would make that integral diverge — which is what happens in the AK model, where diminishing returns are absent. So "level not rate" is a consequence of Assumption 4.3 and of nothing else. Derivation in 05-comparative-statics.md §5.2.
