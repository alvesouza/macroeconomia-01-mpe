## Thesis

One number that no central bank can observe — the income elasticity of money demand —
decides whether it hits its inflation target. Everything else in the lecture is either the
derivation of that number, the equation it sits in, or the machinery that had to be assumed
to get there.

The thesis survives the longer format because the added material *serves* it: the multiplier
explains why the central bank only controls money indirectly, velocity explains why the
famous equation is empty without the elasticity, and seigniorage explains why a government
might want the inflation the elasticity mispredicts.

## The cold open, and its clock

**Second 0–8.** A target line at 2 per cent. A realised line at 0.5 per cent. The gap
labelled *1.5 points*.

**Second 8–20.** Three numbers beside it: the economy grows at 3 per cent, the bank chose
money growth of 3.5 per cent, and it controlled the money stock *exactly*. No shock, no
crisis, no execution error.

**Second 20–30.** On screen: *it did everything right and still missed by 1.5 points — what
did it get wrong?* Then the promise: the answer is one number, and by the end you will know
why that number is one half and not one.

Motivation satisfied with 22 seconds to spare, and deliberately **not** opened on a
definition of money. The definitions arrive in Beat 5, once the viewer wants them.

## The wrong first attempt — three of them

**In the theory (Beats 2–4, paid off in 11).** The natural guess is that money demand is
proportional to income. Beat 3's algebra delivers a *square root* instead, Beat 4 explains
why, and Beat 11 prices the error at exactly 1.5 points.

**In the institutions (Beats 6–8).** Build the multiplier carefully, then break it. The
derivation assumes banks hold the minimum reserves they can, which requires reserves to pay
less than everything else. Remove that and the machine stops: late 2008, the US base rose
almost fivefold while the M1 multiplier fell from about 2 to below 1.

**In the data (Beat 16).** The strongest beat, and it was not designed — it came out of
fetching the series. All 159 countries give correlation **0.171**, slope **0.061**; the
theory looks refuted. One point sits at 3,879 per cent average money growth: **Sierra
Leone**, whose annual series is 7–76 per cent every year *except* **2001 at 131,119 per
cent** and **2015 at −99.89 per cent** — a redenomination or a units break, not an economy;
its median year is 23 per cent. Drop the implausible averages and the cloud becomes Kurlat's
figure: correlation **0.746**, slope **1.064**.

## Closing image

$\pi = \mu - \eta g$ held centre-screen. Beneath it the four numbers from the cold open:
target 2, growth 3, money growth 3.5, realised 0.5. Behind it, faded, the cleaned
cross-country cloud with its 45-degree line. In the corner, $\eta = \tfrac12$ in yellow,
with *Baumol-Tobin* under it.

**Why this frame.** It holds the law, the number that makes it bite, the arithmetic of a real
policy failure, and the evidence that the law is not merely algebra. Redraw this in December
and you have the lecture.

## The novelty line

The slides give you the formula and tell you the elasticity is one half. This video makes the
elasticity *cost something*: it opens on a central bank missing by 1.5 points and spends the
runtime showing the miss was one number wide — then breaks its own machinery twice, once in
2008 and once in the cross-country data, and shows it surviving both.

## Build order and cost

1. `script.md` + `beats.json` → `speechify_tts.py --script`. ≈ **46,000 characters billable**,
   one pass; beats over 2,000 characters chunk automatically on sentence boundaries.
2. `scenes.py`, 20 scenes, each timed from the measured audio.
3. Draft render at `-ql` (854×480, 15 fps) to check timing and composition.
4. `explainer_compile.py` → one MP4, drift under one frame, at draft quality first.

   well over an hour of wall clock. Run it only once the draft reads correctly.

   against the 2 per cent target live. Published as an Artifact.
