Build a slide deck that turns the two derived curves into a working diagram, for Lecture 8 of a graduate macroeconomics course. Deck 2 of 4: deck 1 derived the curves, this one draws, positions, shifts and solves them in closed form, and decks 3 and 4 apply the result.

Sources. "Livro_Benigno_2015_NewKeynesianEconomics_AS_AD_View.pdf" (= BENIGNO), sections 4.2 to 5, printed pages 508 to 511, figures 1 to 5. Also "Livro_Kurlat_Cap10-11_Money_and_Inflation.pdf" (= KURLAT) for one comparison slide: the dichotomy this model keeps in the long run and breaks in the short. Every result slide carries section, equation and printed page.

Thesis the whole deck serves. Two lines and two anchors settle every comparative static in the course, and the commonest error is treating the anchors as equals: the supply anchor is a property of the model, the demand anchor an assumption about policy.

Structure, in this order.

ONE. THE LONG RUN FIRST. Write the long-run natural rate and derive long-run natural consumption from the resource constraint, showing that the coefficient on long-run spending collapses to minus eta over the consumption share times the inverse of sigma plus eta. State three facts as a table: money is neutral, since the long-run price level appears nowhere; fiscal policy is not, since every tax enters the mark-up; and with linear disutility of labour, output rises one-to-one with spending while consumption is unchanged. The third row is itself a proof of the coefficient on g in equation 15.

TWO. WHO SETS WHAT. A table of instrument assignment: monetary policy sets the long-run price level and the short-run nominal rate; fiscal policy sets spending and four tax rates per period; the lump-sum transfer closes the government constraint, equation 18. That transfer is what makes Ricardian equivalence hold through section 9; name the section where it fails.

THREE. THE PLANNER, AND THE ONLY SUBTRACTION THAT MATTERS. Set out the planner problem, substitute both constraints to make it one variable, and take the first-order condition: the marginal rate of substitution equals the marginal rate of transformation. Put it beside equation 14 so the only difference is visibly the mark-up wedge, and reach equation 19. Subtract equation 19 from equation 15 and box the result: natural minus efficient equals minus the mark-up over the inverse of sigma plus eta. Then its consequences: productivity and spending move both levels together, while the mark-up is the unique inefficient shock. Add footnote 21 on the zero-mark-up steady state.

FOUR. THE TWO LINES AND THEIR ANCHORS. One row per curve: slope, anchor, and what moves it. Supply has slope kappa through expected prices and the natural rate. Demand has slope minus one over sigma and passes through that same point only when the nominal rate equals the natural real rate and long-run prices equal expected prices. Quote BENIGNO on page 510: that second condition is an assumption made to simplify the figure.

FIVE. SOLVING THE SYSTEM IN CLOSED FORM. The deck's centre, because the article stops at the picture. Substitute supply into demand on the slide, collect terms, derive output as a function of the instruments. Subtract the natural rate and box two results: the gap equals sigma over one plus sigma-kappa, times the bracket holding the natural real rate, the nominal rate and the long-run price level; and the price surprise is kappa times that gap. Derive the natural real rate on its own slide by imposing a zero gap and stable prices, annotating its four terms and marking the growth term as the one that can turn it negative.

SIX. THE COMPARATIVE-STATICS MACHINE. Differentiate the two boxed results and give the four facts as separate slides with their coefficients: a current supply shock passes through at sigma-kappa over one plus sigma-kappa, always below one; a future shock passes through at one over one plus sigma-kappa and is entirely gap; prices move only with the gap; and the nominal rate and the long-run price level share one slot with opposite signs. Mark the fourth as what deck 4 will use.

SEVEN. WHAT SHIFTS WHAT. Two tables reproducing figures 2 and 4 in full: everything that shifts supply, each shift computed as a new anchor point; and everything that shifts demand, split into short-run instruments and long-run expectations. Work the long-run consumption tax as a case: compute both channels and show the net effect is sigma-eta over the shared denominator, positive but exactly zero when eta vanishes.

EIGHT. WHERE THE TWO MODELS PART. One slide against KURLAT: the classical dichotomy survives in the long run, so the earlier lecture's neutrality result is bounded rather than overturned. One line on why there is no LM curve.

Scope lock. Benigno sections 1 to 5 and the long-run dichotomy from Kurlat chapters 10 and 11. No Calvo staggered pricing, no stochastic DSGE, no Bellman equations, no time-series econometrics, no Kurlat chapter 8 or chapters 12 to 15.
