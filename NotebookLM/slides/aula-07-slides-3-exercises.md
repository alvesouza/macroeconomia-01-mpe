Build a slide deck that works the exercises for Lecture 7 of a graduate macroeconomics course, on money and inflation. This is deck 3 of 4. Decks 1 and 2 derived the theory and did the mechanics; you solve problems.

Sources. Use "Lista_MPE_Macro1_2026_Lista6.pdf" (the problem set, two questions, ten points) and "Livro_Kurlat_Cap10-11_Money_and_Inflation.pdf" (Kurlat chapters 10 and 11, printed pages 189 to 222).

Output language: English. All slide text, labels and equations in English, whatever language the uploaded sources are in.

Thesis the whole deck serves. Every item on this problem set is one equation read in a particular direction, and the marks are in saying which direction and why.

Structure, in this order.

ONE. The problem set, question 1, parts a and b. For part a, two halves on separate slides. First, why the quantity equation is an identity: velocity is defined as nominal output over the money stock, so it is true by construction and cannot fail. Second, the causation question actually asked: the identity constrains the combination of price level, output and velocity but says nothing about which moves or what causes what. Then name the two assumptions that convert it into a theory, a stable velocity and output set by real forces. For part b, derive velocity as output over real money demand, substitute the square-root demand, and obtain velocity as the square root of twice the nominal rate times output over the fixed cost. End on the collision: the model of money demand contradicts the constant-velocity assumption in the dimension that matters.

TWO. Question 1, parts c and d. Part c is the item most answers get wrong by being too confident. Show that with the price level fixed, the condition forces output up or the nominal rate down, then state on its own slide that these chapters contain no mechanism delivering either, because output is pinned by real forces and the price level is fixed by assumption, leaving no free variable. Then answer the flexible-price version with the classical result and the proportional jump. For part d, put the same equation on two slides, once with the money stock exogenous and the price level adjusting, once with the rate set and the money stock endogenous, and state that you cannot choose both independently.

THREE. Question 2, parts a and b. For part a, reproduce the time differentiation: differentiate the equilibrium condition, divide by the condition itself to convert levels into growth rates, define the income elasticity, and kill the interest term using the constant nominal rate. Arrive at inflation equal to money growth minus the elasticity times output growth. For part b, show every substitution. The elasticity under the square-root demand is one half because the exponent of output is one half. With a two per cent target and three per cent growth, money growth must be three and a half per cent; if the true elasticity is one, realised inflation is half a per cent, an undershoot of one and a half percentage points.

FOUR. Question 2, part c, the last and most conceptual item. Derive it by logarithms: take logs of the square-root demand, differentiate with respect to time, use the constant nominal rate to drop that term, and obtain the growth of real money demand as one half of the sum of output growth and cost growth. Combine with the condition in growth rates to get inflation equal to money growth minus one half of that sum. Then devote two slides to the explanation, where the marks are. The fixed cost enters with the same elasticity as output because both sit under the same square root. A falling cost means cheaper trips, so households hold lower real balances, so money demand grows more slowly, so a fixed money growth rule is too loose and inflation rises. State the general principle: inflation is the gap between the growth of nominal money supplied and of real money demanded.
