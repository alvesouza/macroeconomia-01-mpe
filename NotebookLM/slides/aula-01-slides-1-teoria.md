THESIS THIS DECK BUILDS TOWARD. The three ways of measuring national output are not three independent estimates that happen to agree. They are three places to cut the same accounting identity, and every case that appears to break the identity is a case where a convention has to be stated out loud.

STRUCTURE. Aim for 16 to 20 slides. Each title is a full-sentence claim, not a label. Derive every result; assert nothing that can be shown.

BLOCK A - THE CIRCULAR FLOW AS THE FOUNDATION. Open with the flow between firms and households, goods moving one way and payments the other. Establish the three cut points on it and name them: production, income, expenditure. Then prove the coincidence rather than asserting it. Every unit of output sold becomes revenue; every unit of revenue is paid out as wages, rent, interest or profit; every unit paid out is spent or saved. Give the expenditure identity Y = C + I + G + X - M and say which cut it belongs to. Close the block with the consequence that matters: because the three agree by construction, a gap between them in published statistics is measurement error, and agencies publish it as an explicit discrepancy line.

BLOCK B - VALUE ADDED, DERIVED FROM THE PROBLEM IT SOLVES. Do not define value added first. Start with the failure: sum the revenues of a chain of firms and show the intermediate goods being counted more than once. Then derive value added as the correction, and prove the general result that the sum of value added across all firms equals the value of final sales. Show why this is not a coincidence but an algebraic identity, cancelling the intermediate terms explicitly.

BLOCK C - THE INCOME SIDE AND THE ROLE OF PROFIT. Enumerate the factor payments and show that profit is what makes the income side close by construction, since it is defined as the residual after other factors are paid. Draw out the implication: the income method cannot fail to match the production method, because its residual term is defined to absorb any difference. State that this is a strength for consistency and a weakness for independent verification.

BLOCK D - THE BOUNDARY OF PRODUCTION. This is the conceptual core of the deck. Establish that the accounts record market transactions and that this is a convention rather than a discovery. Take three cases and resolve each with the rule and the reason: household production against the same service purchased; government services that have no market price and are therefore valued at cost; and owner-occupied housing, where an imputed rent is invented precisely because the convention would otherwise produce an absurdity. Draw the general lesson: an imputation is an admission that the boundary was drawn in an inconvenient place.

BLOCK E - TRANSFERS ARE NOT OUTPUT. Give the distinction its own block because it is the one students most often lose. A payment that corresponds to no production changes no method's total. Contrast a tax paid out of income with a fee earned for a service and then taxed. Show both entries under all three methods in a small table.

BLOCK F - GDP AGAINST GNP. Define both precisely, give the relation through net factor income from abroad, and state which one is a statement about territory and which about ownership. Name one country where the two diverge materially and give the structural reason in a sentence.

BLOCK G - WHAT THE THEORY DOES NOT SETTLE. Close by listing the three choices the framework leaves open, which the remaining decks will take up: where the production boundary falls, which prices supply the weights when comparing across time, and whose welfare the resulting number is meant to track. State that none of the three is resolved by collecting better data.
