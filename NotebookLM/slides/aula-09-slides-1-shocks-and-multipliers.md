Build a slide deck that works every comparative static of the New-Keynesian AS-AD model, for Lecture 9 of a graduate macroeconomics course. Deck 3 of 4: decks 1 and 2 derived and drew the curves, this one runs the shocks and the fiscal multipliers, deck 4 covers the zero bound, deleveraging and optimal policy.

Source. "Livro_Benigno_2015_NewKeynesianEconomics_AS_AD_View.pdf" (= BENIGNO), sections 6 to 8, printed pages 511 to 516, figures 5 to 10, tables 1 and 2. Every result slide carries section, equation and printed page.

Thesis the whole deck serves. Each shock is answered by one question — does it separate the natural level of output from the efficient level? — and each fiscal instrument by two numbers that can disagree in sign: its multiplier on output and its multiplier on the gap.

Structure, in this order.

ONE. THE METHOD. Five steps: recompute the natural rate from equation 15 and redraw supply through its anchor; ask whether the shock touches demand; find the new equilibrium; read both gaps; name the policy that restores the optimum. Then the closed-form gap from deck 2, so each case below is one substitution rather than a picture.

TWO. PRODUCTIVITY, THREE CASES. One slide per case: the shifted curves, the signed gap, the price change and the required rate move, as expressions and not adjectives.
Temporary, section 6.1: supply shifts, demand does not; output rises by sigma-kappa over one plus sigma-kappa times the change in the natural rate; the gap is negative at minus one over one plus sigma-kappa; prices fall; policy cuts the rate by the shock over sigma, which equals the change in the natural real rate.
Permanent, section 6.2: both curves shift, the gap is exactly zero, prices do not move, and the natural real rate is unchanged, since it depends on the difference between long-run and current natural output.
Expected, section 6.3: only demand shifts, the whole response is a gap, prices rise, policy tightens.
Then a summary table with the three cases in rows and columns for each curve, the gap, prices, the natural real rate and the policy sign.

THREE. WHY THERE IS NO TRADE-OFF, PROVED. Derive the divine coincidence rather than asserting it: write the loss function, substitute the supply curve, and show both terms vanish together if and only if the natural and efficient levels coincide — that is, if and only if the mark-up is unchanged. Caveats: wage flexibility, footnote 9; efficient steady state, footnote 21.

FOUR. MARK-UP SHOCKS. List their sources from equation 13, including an inelastically demanded input such as oil. The natural rate falls; the efficient level does not move. Then the slide that carries the section: the two gaps have opposite signs — positive against the natural rate, hence rising prices, negative against the efficient rate, hence falling welfare. Present figure 9's three reachable points as a table — doing nothing, price stability, efficient output — each with the rate move it needs and the resulting price surprise and efficient gap. The objectives need moves of opposite sign, which is what a trade-off means.

FIVE. FISCAL MULTIPLIERS, DERIVED. Substitute the supply curve into the fiscal terms of demand, then the fiscal content of both natural rates, and read off coefficients one at a time. Derive all six entries of table 1, and show the cancellation producing eta in the numerator of the long-run spending multiplier — a third proof of the coefficient on g in equation 15. Give the calibration and where each number comes from.

SIX. TABLE 2, REPRODUCED AND READ. Show the eight-row table, then three readings: every multiplier is below one; the spending multiplier equals one only at eta of zero, the no-crowding-out knife edge; and the consumption-tax multiplier is exactly sigma times it, which is why it swings most with the elasticity of substitution.

SEVEN. THE MULTIPLIERS ON THE GAP. Derive equation 23 by subtracting equation 15, with all three cancellations. Three results: permanent fiscal policy cannot move the gap, since every term is a short-minus-long difference; the gap multipliers are the long-run output multipliers, an order of magnitude smaller; and a current consumption-tax cut raises output while widening the gap. Add the gap against the efficient level, and note that the prose on page 515 has the correspondence backwards — tax multipliers as in equation 22, spending multipliers as in equation 23.

EIGHT. THE PICTURE, FIGURE 10. A temporary spending rise moves both curves. Show why the new equilibrium sits right of the new natural rate, quote the sentence about an economy overheated while private consumption is crowded out, give the stabilising rate move, and close on footnote 10's knife edge at eta of zero.

Scope lock. Benigno sections 6 to 8. No Calvo staggered pricing, no stochastic DSGE, no Bellman equations, no time-series econometrics. The zero bound, deleveraging and optimal policy are deck 4.
