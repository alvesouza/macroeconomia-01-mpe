Thesis the whole deck serves. One equilibrium condition, read four ways, produces every quantitative result in this lecture. The work is deciding which variable is free to move.

ONE. The condition, and its three channels. State money market equilibrium as the money supply equal to real money demand times the price level. Then put on one slide the three and only three ways the market can clear after a rise in the money supply: the price level rises, the nominal interest rate falls, or output rises, each with its mechanism in one line. State the classical view and show that it closes two of the three, so the price level must adjust. Make clear that neutrality is not an extra assumption added at the end; it is the statement that two channels are shut.

TWO. Measurement, as a worked computation. Compute a GDP deflator and a consumer price index from the lecture's own tables, reproducing both arithmetics in full: the wheat and computers example giving a deflator near 73 and inflation of about minus 27 per cent, and the three-luxury-good basket going from 300 to 330, so the index goes from 100 to 110 and inflation is 10 per cent. State what each index weights by, and give the oil exporter as the case where the two diverge.

THREE. Fisher, exactly and approximately. Derive one plus the real rate as one plus the nominal rate over one plus inflation, then the subtraction approximation, and say when it fails. Work the lecture's example: an eleven per cent nominal rate, two per cent expected inflation, an index going from 100 to 102, a hundred dollars buying one basket and a hundred and eleven repaid buying about 1.088 baskets, so the real rate is about 8.8 per cent. Separate ex-ante from ex-post, and add the rule for which rate belongs where: real for intertemporal goods decisions, nominal for money holding.

FOUR. Three steady states, in sequence. With a constant money supply, solve the condition for the price level. With money growing at a constant rate, conjecture that inflation equals that rate and verify the condition holds period by period. Then the growing economy: differentiate the condition with respect to time, divide through by the condition itself to turn levels into growth rates, define the income elasticity of money demand, and show the interest term vanishes once inflation is constant. Arrive at inflation equal to money growth minus the elasticity times output growth.

FIVE. Signing the comparative statics. One slide per sign, each with its reason rather than only the derivative. Inflation rises one for one with money growth; falls with output growth, because a growing economy absorbs money without prices rising; and falls with the elasticity, which measures how much money growth real growth soaks up. Then the two experiments, the second being the hard one. A one-off rise in the level of money raises the price level proportionally. A permanent rise in the growth rate raises inflation and also makes the price level jump at the moment of the change, because the higher nominal rate lowers desired real balances while the nominal stock has not yet changed.

SIX. Velocity, as an implication rather than an assumption. State the quantity equation and label it a definition, true by the way velocity is defined. Derive velocity as output over real money demand. Substitute the square-root money demand to obtain velocity as the square root of twice the nominal interest rate times output over the fixed cost. Close with the pivot: assuming velocity constant is what converts the identity into the quantity theory, and the model just derived says velocity is not constant whenever the interest rate moves.

Include one runnable Python block, numpy only: define Baumol-Tobin real balances and velocity, compute the income and interest elasticities by finite differences and assert they match one half and minus one half, then solve for the money growth hitting a two per cent target under three per cent growth with an elasticity of one half, and the inflation if the true elasticity is one.
