Build a slide deck that derives the theory of money and inflation for Lecture 7 of a graduate macroeconomics course. This is deck 1 of 4. It derives; deck 2 computes and signs derivatives; deck 3 works exercises; deck 4 connects the lecture to the rest of the course.

Sources. Use "Aula_MPE_Macro1_SlidesAula7_2026.pdf" (the lecture, 22 slides, 8 sections) and "Livro_Kurlat_Cap10-11_Money_and_Inflation.pdf" (Kurlat chapters 10 and 11 isolated, printed pages 189 to 222). Where the lecture and the book differ in notation, follow the book and say so on the slide.

Output language: English. All slide text, labels and equations in English, whatever language the uploaded sources are in.

Thesis the whole deck serves. Money demand is not an assumption in this theory. It is the solution to a cost-minimisation problem, and every result about inflation downstream inherits the elasticities that problem produces.

Structure, in this order.

ONE. What money is, and why there is no single measure. The three functions, and an explicit argument that the medium of exchange is the one that defines money because it is the one that solves the double coincidence of wants. Then the five properties, each with its reason rather than as a bare list. Close on the consequence: because those properties are satisfied by degrees, no sharp line exists between money and not money. Present the ladder as a table: base as currency plus reserves, M0 as currency, M1 as currency plus demand deposits, M2 as M1 plus savings deposits, small time deposits and money market fund shares. State that the base is not a smaller M1 but a different object, because it counts reserves no household holds.

TWO. The supply of money. Give the commercial bank balance sheet, with reserves named as an asset of the bank and a liability of the central bank. Give both reasons banks hold reserves, unexpected withdrawals and regulation, and say which one dominates today and why the other faded. Then build the three-agent example from the book, central bank, one commercial bank, one household, and derive in the notation of the source that the monetary base is currency plus the reserve ratio times deposits while M1 is currency plus the whole of deposits. Point at that gap as the origin of the multiplier.

THREE. The multiplier, derived rather than asserted. Walk the open market operation as a numbered sequence: the central bank buys bonds with newly created reserves, the bank holds excess reserves, expands loans, the borrower deposits, a fraction is withdrawn as cash, and the round repeats scaled down. Show the geometric series and sum it to equation 10.3.1. Put one correction on its own slide, in the book's terms: reserves do not disappear when banks lend; banks take advantage of the relaxation of the reserve-to-deposit ratio to expand loans and deposits. Name which argument is a policy instrument and which is public behaviour.

FOUR. Where the multiplier breaks. Derive the condition: the whole construction assumed banks hold the minimum possible reserves, which requires reserves to pay less than other assets. Show that at a zero interest rate, or when reserves are remunerated, banks willingly hold excess reserves and changes in the base stop passing through to M1. Use the 2008 episode from the sources with its magnitudes.

FIVE. Baumol-Tobin, derived in full. Set out the two assets, spending spread evenly over the period, and the fixed real cost of a trip to the bank. Draw the sawtooth path of money holdings and read the average balance off it. Write the cost function with both terms named, trip cost and foregone interest. Take the first-order condition, solve for the optimal number of trips, substitute back, and arrive at real balances equal to the square root of output times the fixed cost over twice the nominal rate. State the three comparative statics with their reasons.

SIX. The elasticities, and the distinction that controls every later problem. From the square root, read off an income elasticity of one half, an interest elasticity of minus one half, and an elasticity of one half with respect to the fixed cost. Explain the one half as economies of scale in cash management: doubling income does not double money holdings, because the household makes more trips. Then draw the line between the square-root function and the generalised money demand function, merely increasing in income and decreasing in the rate, and state which results may be used with which.

Scope lock. Kurlat chapters 1 to 7, 9, 10 and 11 only. No Bellman equations, dynamic programming, stochastic DSGE, Calvo pricing or time-series econometrics. Chapter 8 and chapters 12 to 15 are outside this course; aggregate demand and supply is Lectures 8 and 9.
