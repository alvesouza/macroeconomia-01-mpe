Thesis, and the conversation serves only this. In this course the algebra is usually right and the mark is lost in the words beneath it. Four habits recover it: answer every object the question names; make the words carry the sign the formula carries; when you have proved something, write the conclusion it proves; and when asked to explain, give one causal chain from the change to the result.

Four segments. Do not add a fifth.

SEGMENT ONE. COUNT THE OBJECTS, THEN READ THE SIGN. Open with the habit that costs the most marks and takes the least thought. LISTA 3 asks for first-period consumption, second-period consumption and saving; an answer that stops at the first two is incomplete however good it is. Have one host list the nouns in the question before anyone solves anything. Then the sign. LISTA 3 part c removes future income and taxes and asks how first-period consumption responds to the interest rate. KURLAT's answer turns on the curvature parameter. Above one, the income effect dominates: a saver is richer when the rate rises, and it spends part of that today, so first-period consumption rises. Below one, substitution dominates and it falls. At one, the log case, the two cancel. The autopsy: a graded answer wrote the right condition and then said consumption falls. Teach the check: set the parameter to the knife edge and read the formula before writing the sentence. Another episode drills that habit for labour; here it is enough to apply it once.

SEGMENT TWO. A PROOF ENDS IN ITS CONCLUSION. LISTA 3 part e asks the student to show that taxes matter only through their present value, and then asks whether their timing matters. The graded answer proved the first and then said yes, timing matters, citing the previous item. Have one host defend that answer; let the other show it confuses two experiments: part d changes the level of taxes, part e moves the same present value across time. The conclusion the proof delivers: cut today's tax by one unit and raise tomorrow's by that unit plus interest, and neither period's consumption changes, while saving rises by exactly the tax cut, set aside to pay the later tax. Timing does not matter, as long as the household can borrow and lend freely at the market rate. One sentence only on where that breaks; another episode covers the borrowing limit.

SEGMENT THREE. EXPLAIN MEANS ONE CAUSAL CHAIN. This is the segment the episode exists for; give it the most room. LISTA 6 part c: the fixed cost of a trip to the bank falls steadily, money growth is unchanged, and inflation turns out higher. Build the chain aloud, one link per host turn. Trips get cheaper, so people make more of them and hold less cash on average, as KURLAT's section on money demand shows. Money demand therefore grows more slowly. With money supply growing at the same rate, supply now outruns demand. Prices must rise faster to bring real balances down to what people want to hold. Inflation is higher. Then the autopsy: the graded answer said money became cheaper to obtain, so there is more inflation. That names the cost of getting money and never mentions demand, so the link that does the work is missing. Another episode explains this mechanism; here the point is the shape of the sentence.

SEGMENT FOUR. THE REHEARSAL. Have the hosts write the three lost answers aloud as a grader would want them, each in at most three sentences. The interest-rate item: the sign, the condition on curvature, the effect that dominates and why a saver is richer. The tax-timing item: consumption in both periods unchanged, saving up by the tax cut, timing irrelevant under free borrowing and lending. The trip-cost item: the full causal chain from the cheaper trip to higher inflation, with money demand named in the middle. After each, have the other host check it against the formula on the page and point to the clause that would have lost the mark.

Skip entirely: the derivation of the Euler equation, the algebra of the square-root money demand, the savings tax, and the calibration of the income elasticity of money demand. Stay at the level of the uploaded material; if a source goes further, register it in one sentence and move on.

One instruction that belongs to audio and not to slides: before every sentence of interpretation, have one host state the sign or result the formula gives, and the other refuse to let the interpretation go on air until it agrees. Never read an expression aloud; say what it decides.
