Produce a two-host conversation about deriving the golden-rule saving rate without losing the exponent. Source: "Livro_Pablo_Kurlat_-_A_Course_in_Modern_Macroeconomics_2020_-_libgen.li.pdf" (= KURLAT, section 4.3 at printed pages 61 to 63, equations 4.3.1 to 4.3.4 and the Cobb-Douglas case closing page 63; section 4.4 on factor markets at pages 63 to 69; factor shares at pages 75 and 76).

Thesis, and the conversation serves only this. The golden-rule saving rate equals the capital share, and there are two routes to it that must agree: maximise steady-state consumption over the saving rate, carrying the capital exponent through the substitution, or set the marginal product of capital equal to depreciation plus population growth. An answer that lands anywhere else fails a one-line test: let the capital share go to zero and see whether the saving rate goes to zero with it.

Four segments. Do not add a fifth.

SEGMENT ONE. TWO CHANNELS. Start from KURLAT's steady-state consumption: whatever output is not saved. The saving rate enters twice. Directly, it takes a larger bite of output. Indirectly, it raises steady-state capital and so raises output. Have one host state the trade-off in words before any algebra: the golden rule is the saving rate at which the two channels exactly offset. Then KURLAT's restatement: choose the capital stock instead of the saving rate, and consumption becomes output minus the investment needed to keep capital per worker constant.

SEGMENT TWO. THE ERROR AUTOPSY: THE MISSING EXPONENT. This is the segment the episode exists for; give it the most room. The graded answer had steady-state capital right: the saving rate enters it with the power one over one minus alpha. Consumption is one minus the saving rate times output, and output is capital raised to alpha, so inside consumption the saving rate carries the power alpha over one minus alpha. The graded answer dropped the alpha, kept one over one minus alpha, and the first-order condition then returned one over two minus alpha. Have one host walk the substitution power by power, and the other stop the walk at the exact step where the two powers must be multiplied. Then finish it in words: take logs, so the objective is the log of one minus the saving rate plus alpha over one minus alpha times the log of the saving rate; set the derivative to zero; cross-multiply; the saving rate equals alpha. One sentence on the second-order condition: both terms are concave, so this is a maximum.

SEGMENT THREE. THE SECOND ROUTE, AND A TEST THAT CAN FAIL. KURLAT's equation 4.3.4: at the golden rule, the marginal product of capital equals depreciation plus population growth. The saving rate that sustains a capital stock is depreciation plus growth times capital, over output; under Cobb-Douglas that ratio is exactly alpha. Connect it to section 4.4: in competitive factor markets capital is paid its marginal product, so alpha is capital's share of income, the share KURLAT shows is roughly constant. The rule in one line: save capital's income, consume labour's. Then the knife edge. One host proposes testing at alpha equal to one and finds that the right and the wrong formula both pass. Let the other explain why a test has to be run where the candidates disagree: as alpha goes to zero capital is useless, so the right saving rate must go to zero; the correct answer gives zero, the wrong one gives one half.

SEGMENT FOUR. THE REHEARSAL. Have the hosts write the lost answer as a grader would want it, in at most five sentences: consumption as unsaved output; the substitution with the power alpha over one minus alpha; the first-order condition and its solution; the cross-check through the marginal product; the reading, golden-rule saving equals the capital share, and an economy saving more has higher output but lower consumption. After it, the other host runs the knife-edge test on the final formula aloud.

Skip entirely: technological progress, growth accounting, the interest-rate calibration of chapter 5, and the transition path between steady states. Stay at the level of the uploaded material; if a source goes further, register it in one sentence and move on.

One instruction that belongs to audio and not to slides: before any formula is accepted, one host must say what it predicts when the capital share goes to zero, and the other refuses to continue until that prediction makes economic sense. Say powers as fractions of alpha in words, never as a string of symbols.
