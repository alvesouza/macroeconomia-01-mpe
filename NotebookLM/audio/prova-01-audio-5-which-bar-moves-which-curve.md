Produce a two-host conversation about placing a shock on the right curve of the New-Keynesian AS-AD diagram, and reading the sign of policy from it. Source: "Livro_Benigno_2015_NewKeynesianEconomics_AS_AD_View.pdf" (= BENIGNO, sections four to seven at printed pages 506 to 514: the long run on page 508, the efficient level on page 509, what moves AD on page 510, optimism about future productivity on page 513; section eleven at pages 521 to 523).

Thesis, and the conversation serves only this. In BENIGNO's model every variable with a bar is a long-run object and moves AD, through expected future consumption; every current variable without a bar moves AS, through the natural level of output. Once the shock sits on the right curve the sign of policy follows. A pure demand shock carries no trade-off, so the central bank moves the rate to undo it, by the same amount whatever weight it puts on prices.

Four segments. Do not add a fifth.

SEGMENT ONE. WHICH BAR MOVES WHICH CURVE. AS ties today's prices to output, anchored at natural output, which depends on the current mark-up. AD is the Euler equation: today's consumption rises with expected long-run consumption, which depends on long-run productivity, long-run spending and the long-run mark-up. The error autopsy: the graded answer shifted AS for news of a higher long-run mark-up. Have one host defend it: a mark-up is a supply object. Let the other take it apart: the long-run mark-up lowers future natural output; households expect to be poorer, smooth, and cut consumption now; AD shifts left. The current mark-up has not moved, so AS stays put. BENIGNO's section on optimism about future productivity is the mirror image: AD moves, AS does not.

SEGMENT TWO. THE SIGN OF POLICY. With AD to the left, output and prices both fall and the gap opens below natural output. Price stability means returning to the expected price, which on AS means returning to natural output, so AD must come back: cut the nominal rate. The graded answer raised it, which deepens both the recession and the fall in prices. The cost in terms of the gap is none, because a pure demand shock carries no trade-off, what the literature calls the divine coincidence; BENIGNO draws the contrast with mark-up shocks, which do carry one. Then what policy cannot cure: natural output stays below efficient output because of the mark-up wedge, and long-run output is lower for good. One sentence only: with the rate near zero the cut may not be available.

SEGMENT THREE. OPTIMAL POLICY LIVES ON AS. This is the segment the episode exists for; give it the most room. BENIGNO's loss weighs the gap from efficient output and the deviation of prices. The central bank moves AD through the rate, so in effect it chooses a point on AS: substitute AS into the loss and minimise over output, which gives the targeting rule of section eleven. The autopsy: the graded answer differentiated with respect to the rate, used an efficient output that contained the mark-up, and concluded that the response depends on the price weight against the intertemporal elasticity of substitution. Take it apart: the news moves neither efficient nor natural output today, so the targeted point does not move, and the bank offsets the whole AD shift whatever the weight. Show where the comparison the student remembered really lives: BENIGNO compares that weight with the same elasticity to say whether the targeting line is flatter than AD, a fact about the drawing, not about this response. The weight decides only where on AS the economy sits.

SEGMENT FOUR. THE REHEARSAL. Have the hosts write the three lost answers aloud, each in at most three sentences. The shift: AD left, AS fixed, output and prices down, and why. The policy: a cut, no trade-off, and what remains uncured. The optimum: the targeting rule along AS, a point that does not move, and an offset independent of the weight. After each, the other host names the curve that did not move and why.

Skip entirely: fiscal multipliers, the liquidity trap and debt deleveraging beyond the one sentence above, the Taylor rule and its coefficients, and the derivation of the loss from utility. Stay at the level of the uploaded material; if a source goes further, register it in one sentence and move on. Other episodes build the loss-and-tangency picture and the divine coincidence; here they serve only the three answers.
