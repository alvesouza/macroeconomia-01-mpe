SEGMENT ONE. WHAT IT OWNS: THE MISSING PROFITS. The household starts with an asset, has a unit of time, and owns the firm. The graded budget had the asset and the wage bill and left out the firm's profit. Have one host object with KURLAT's page 166: profits are zero in equilibrium, so why carry them? Drop it and capital's share of output vanishes from the household's wealth, and labour income no longer adds up to output. Verdict: one of the two, rent or profit, must be in the budget.

Here work carries no disutility: each hour adds the wage to income and costs nothing in utility, so the marginal value of an hour is strictly positive at any interior point, and the household supplies its whole endowment. Writing that, with a positive multiplier on the time constraint, is the first-order condition for labour. Contrast KURLAT's equation 9.1.12: when leisure is valued, the marginal rate of substitution equals the marginal product of labour, an interior condition; remove leisure from utility and its left side is zero, so no interior point satisfies it.
