Thesis. In the long run the interest rate of this economy is impatience and nothing else. Not productivity, not the capital share, not the depreciation rate. Technology gets no vote on the price and complete control of the quantity, and the ownership question sitting next to it in the same problem is the same fact wearing different clothes.

Scope. Stay at the level of AULA6 and KURLAT9 section 9.3, in the steady state. Do no algebra aloud, and do not describe the phase diagram, which cannot be seen. Where a source goes past the uploaded lecture, note it in one sentence and move on.

Skip entirely: the Golden Rule comparison, the transition path toward the steady state, and everything in question one.

SEGMENT ONE. THE QUESTION A CAREFUL OBSERVER GETS WRONG. Open with two countries that differ only in productivity, and ask which one has the higher interest rate in the long run. Have one host give the answer that sounds obvious: capital is more productive there, so it must pay more, and savers should earn more. Let the other say what the model actually delivers — the same rate in both, with the entire difference showing up as a larger stock of capital. Establish that this is the shape of question the course keeps returning to, where a competent person with ordinary data concludes the wrong thing, and hold the resolution back.

SEGMENT TWO. WHY THE PRICE IS A PREFERENCE. Deliver the argument in its shortest form. A steady state means consumption is the same every period. The household's intertemporal condition says that a flat path is chosen only when the reward for waiting exactly offsets impatience, neither more nor less. Have one host point out what is absent from that sentence: no production function, no capital share, no depreciation, nothing physical at all. Have the other press on whether this is a trick of the assumptions, and let the first answer that any model with a discount factor and a constant path gives the same thing.

SEGMENT THREE. WHERE THE TECHNOLOGY WENT. This is the core of the episode. If preferences have already fixed the rate, something else must do the adjusting, and it is the quantity of capital. Have the hosts trace the productivity increase step by step: capital is briefly worth more than the fixed rate requires, so the economy accumulates, and accumulating drives the marginal product of capital back down until it meets the number preferences demanded. Have one host state the slogan in exactly these words, preferences determine the interest rate and technology determines the capital stock, and have the other test it by moving depreciation instead and checking that the rate again holds still.

SEGMENT FOUR. WHO OWNS THE MACHINES. Turn to the ownership item. In one arrangement the household owns the capital, rents it to the producer and does the investing itself; in the other a separate firm builds and holds the capital and rents it out. Have the hosts establish that both deliver the same condition: a unit of capital pays its rent and survives depreciation, a bond pays interest, and since people hold both, the two returns must be equal. Name the result as Fisher separation, and say what it rules out: that packaging a claim on a real asset differently could change anything real. Then have one host raise the error the item is built to catch, looking for the investment firm's optimal quantity. Let the other show there is no such thing — the condition pins down a price, the firm is indifferent across every quantity at that price, and the quantity comes from goods market clearing instead.

SEGMENT FIVE. THE TWO SENTENCES TO CARRY INTO THE EXAM. Close by having the hosts state the two results in the form that can be written under time pressure. First, that the long-run rate depends on the discount factor alone, with the reason attached rather than asserted. Second, that ownership of capital is an accounting arrangement and not an economic one, because arbitrage strips the intermediary of any profit and hands the trade-off back to the household unchanged. Have one host note that both answers are one line plus a reason, and that the reason carries the marks. Give the listener one question to carry: if a country's real interest rate is persistently high, which of the two — its preferences or its technology — does this model tell them to look at?
