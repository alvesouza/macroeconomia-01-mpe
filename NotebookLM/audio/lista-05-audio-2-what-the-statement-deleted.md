Thesis. Half the marks on a general equilibrium question are decided before any algebra, by reading which sentence of the statement removed which equation. The two questions in this set are the textbook chapter with different pieces taken out, and the commonest way to lose marks is to write down, out of habit, a market the economy no longer has.

Scope. Stay at the level of AULA6 and KURLAT9. Do no algebra aloud; name the equations by what they say. Where a source goes past the uploaded lecture, note it in one sentence and move on.

Skip entirely: the sign of the hours response, the steady state and the Golden Rule, and the welfare theorem and its proof.

SEGMENT ONE. TWO ECONOMIES BUILT BY SUBTRACTION. Open on the shape of the problem set. The chapter has three agents and two margins; question one freezes capital and keeps the labour margin, question two freezes labour and keeps the capital margin. Neither asks for the whole model at once. Have one host say that removing pieces obviously makes a question easier. Let the other reframe it — it makes the question shorter, not easier, because the marks move from grinding out conditions to knowing which ones are still there.

SEGMENT TWO. THE SENTENCE AND THE EQUATION. Run the drill live on the opening sentence of question one, which says there is no investment technology, that capital is fixed at the same level in both periods, and that it does not depreciate. Have the hosts take the clauses one at a time and name what each one removes: the third agent of the chapter, whose whole business was converting goods into capital; the accumulation equation, which was the only thing physically linking the two dates; and the investment term in goods market clearing, so that consumption now equals output date by date. Have one host state the consequence that carries the rest of the problem — nothing can be moved between the two periods at all.

SEGMENT THREE. THE IMPORT THAT COSTS MARKS. This is the core of the episode. Have one host describe a student who writes the household's wealth by copying it from the chapter, where it includes the rent on capital and the capital itself. Have the other explain why the second piece cannot be there: in the chapter capital can be sold back as goods, and here there is no technology that converts it in either direction, so the household owns something it can rent out and can never eat. Then give the test that settles it without argument. Constant returns to scale means the payments to labour and capital exactly exhaust what was produced, so household income already equals output, which is what goods clearing says consumption equals. Add the capital itself on top and the household can afford more than the economy made. Have the hosts agree that this is the cleanest kind of check, one that uses only accounting.

SEGMENT FOUR. THE SAME LETTER, TWO DIFFERENT JOBS. Take the interest rate across the two questions. In the first there is no storage and no investment, so it cannot move a single good between the dates; it exists only to make the household content with a consumption path it is powerless to change. In the second, capital can be built, so arbitrage ties it to the marginal product of capital net of depreciation and it genuinely allocates. Have one host argue that the first barely deserves the name of a price, and let the other defend it as a shadow price that reports rather than allocates. Land the distinction in one sentence each way.

SEGMENT FIVE. THE MARKET NOBODY HAS TO LIST. Close on the credit market. Have the hosts show that it never needs to appear in the definition of equilibrium: take the household's first-period constraint, use zero profits and the fact that factor payments exhaust output, compare with goods clearing, and net lending has to be zero. Three markets clearing force the fourth, which is Walras's law. Have one host offer the quicker argument, that a single representative household has nobody to trade with, and let the other point out that the accounting version is the one that survives when there is more than one type of household. Give the listener one question to carry: in the next model they meet, which sentence of the setup is doing the deleting?
