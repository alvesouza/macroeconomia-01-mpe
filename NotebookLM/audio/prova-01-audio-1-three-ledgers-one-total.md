Produce a two-host conversation about why every GDP answer must be computed three ways, and why the three must agree. Source: "Livro_Pablo_Kurlat_-_A_Course_in_Modern_Macroeconomics_2020_-_libgen.li.pdf" (= KURLAT, section 1.1 at printed pages 15 to 22: the three approaches on page 16, value added on page 17, forms of investment and durables on pages 17 to 19, services valued at cost on page 20 and non-market activity on pages 21 and 22; section 2.2 from page 33).

Thesis, and the conversation serves only this. GDP is one number kept in three ledgers: the value each producer adds, the incomes those producers pay out, and the final uses of what was produced. An answer is finished only when all three columns are filled and reach the same total, because the agreement is the check: an item placed in the wrong column breaks it at once.

Four segments. Do not add a fifth.

SEGMENT ONE. THREE LEDGERS, ONE TOTAL. Build the table from KURLAT's fertilizer-and-lettuce example. Have one host count the fertilizer twice, once as the plant's sales and once inside the price of the lettuce, and the other take it back out. The rule that comes out: whatever is used up in production is intermediate, and it appears only as a deduction from the buyer's value added. Close by saying aloud that the three totals matched, and that the match is the thing being checked, not a coincidence.

SEGMENT TWO. A SERVICE A FIRM BUYS, A SERVICE USERS GET FREE. A final-goods firm buys advertising from a platform that gives its users a service at no charge. Walk it through the three ledgers. The advertising is an input of the firm that buys it, so it is intermediate: the platform's value added rises and the buyer's falls by the same amount. The free service has no price and no market transaction, so it adds nothing. Total GDP does not move; only the split of income between the two firms moves. Then one host objects that users plainly gain something. Let the other place that gain where KURLAT places it: among the non-market activities that GDP leaves out, and among the things section 2.2 asks a welfare measure to count.

SEGMENT THREE. THE ERROR AUTOPSY: SOFTWARE IS NOT CONSUMPTION. This is the segment the episode exists for; give it the most room. The platform now pays its own workers to build software it will use for years. The graded answer found the right new total and then said the extra value was "wages and consumption". Take it apart one ledger at a time. Production: the platform made something new, valued at what it cost, the same convention KURLAT uses for government services that have no price. Income: the workers' wages, plus the platform's surplus. Expenditure: no household bought anything new, and the statement leaves every other purchase unchanged, so consumption cannot move. The only column left to absorb the new output is investment, and it belongs there by KURLAT's own test: software is produced now and used for production in future periods. Have one host raise KURLAT's durables puzzle: a household's television also lasts for years, yet it is counted as consumption. Let the other give the convention: what decides the column is who uses the good and for what, and an asset a firm keeps in order to produce is investment. Wages are an income item and consumption is a use; naming both for the same hundred mixes two ledgers. One sentence only on the alternative: if the software were expensed as a cost, GDP would not change at all.

SEGMENT FOUR. THE REHEARSAL. Have the hosts write the two lost answers aloud, each as three short lines, one per approach, each ending with its total. The advertising case: value added firm by firm, incomes firm by firm, final consumption; the same total as before, with income moved between firms. The software case: value added including the software, wages plus surplus, consumption unchanged plus investment. After each, the other host checks that the three totals agree and names the line a grader would have marked missing.

Skip entirely: nominal versus real GDP, price indices, purchasing power parity, and the welfare formula of section 2.2. Stay at the level of the uploaded material; if a source goes further, register it in one sentence and move on.

One instruction that belongs to audio and not to slides: whenever a host places an item in one ledger, the other must say where the same item sits in each of the other two before the conversation moves on. Never read a table aloud; say which column moved and why.
