Produce a two-host conversation about the search model of unemployment and the one externality at its centre. Sources: "Livro_Pablo_Kurlat_-_A_Course_in_Modern_Macroeconomics_2020_-_libgen.li.pdf" (= KURLAT, the Beveridge curve at printed page 130, section 7.5 at pages 142 to 150, and Exercise 7.7 on page 150), "Aula_Slides_Macro1_Aula5.pdf" (= AULA5, the section on labour market equilibrium) and "Lista_MPE_Macro1_2026_Lista4.pdf" (= LISTA 4, question two).

Thesis, and the conversation serves only this. Every answer in the search model is about a flow between people other than the one making the decision. The Beveridge curve is the set of unemployment and vacancy pairs at which job creation equals job destruction. A firm that posts a vacancy helps workers and hurts other firms, and ignores both. A fall in matching efficiency moves the whole curve, which is a different event from a slide along it.

Four segments. Do not add a fifth.

SEGMENT ONE. CREATION EQUALS DESTRUCTION. Start with the words, before any algebra. Each period some employed workers lose their jobs, and some unemployed workers meet a vacancy and are hired. In a steady state those two flows are equal, and that condition, stated in those words, is the Beveridge curve. Then the slope, as a story about flows. With more unemployment there are fewer employed, so fewer separations to replace, and more searchers, so each vacancy fills more easily. Both mean fewer vacancies are needed to keep the flows balanced, so the curve slopes down. Have one host say the slope is negative because the derivative is negative; let the other answer that the sign is the result and the flow story is the explanation the question asks for.

SEGMENT TWO. ONE MORE VACANCY. This is the segment the episode exists for; give it the most room. Tightness is vacancies per unemployed worker. When it rises, the job-finding rate rises and the job-filling rate falls; the first equals tightness times the second. Now one firm posts one extra vacancy. Ask the list's question: who gains and who loses? Make the hosts name three agents. The posting firm gains, or it would not post. Workers gain: they find jobs faster. Other firms lose: their vacancies take longer to fill. Add that the extra vacancy creates less than one extra match, and the shortfall is the congestion. The firm weighs only its own expected profit, so it ignores both the harm to rival firms and the benefit to workers. Then the autopsy of a graded answer that said the firm ignores the effect because its own loss is tiny. Wrong agent: a cost that falls on yourself is internalised by definition. An externality is always a cost or benefit that lands on somebody else.

SEGMENT THREE. SHIFT VERSUS MOVEMENT. Take a fall in matching efficiency. At any given unemployment rate the same vacancies now produce fewer hires, so more vacancies are needed to balance the flows: the whole curve moves out. A movement along the curve is the business cycle with the matching technology fixed: tightness changes and the economy slides between more vacancies with less unemployment and the reverse. Have one host argue that more vacancies at the same unemployment is just a movement; let the other show that no point on the old curve has that pair. Close with KURLAT's use of the US curve: an outward shift is read as matching getting worse, not as a boom.

SEGMENT FOUR. WHAT THE GRADER WANTED TO READ. Rehearse, in the hosts' own words, the three answers LISTA 4 question two cost. Part b: name the condition as job creation equals job destruction, then explain the slope in flows, not in signs. Part d: a list, not a sentence: the posting firm gains, workers gain, other firms lose, and the firm internalises neither the harm nor the benefit. Part e: the fall in efficiency shifts every feasible pair of unemployment and vacancies outward, which is not the same as changing how a given tightness is split between the two. For each, have one host say the tempting short version and the other add the clause that earns the mark.

Skip entirely: wage bargaining, reservation wages, unemployment insurance, and the static labour supply choice, which has its own episode. Stay at the level of the uploaded lecture; if a source goes further, for instance on whether the market posts too many or too few vacancies, register it in one sentence and move on.

One instruction that belongs to audio and not to slides: narrate each change as a sequence of events between named people, a firm, a rival firm, a jobseeker, so that the listener hears who carries each cost. Never read an expression aloud; say what it decides.
