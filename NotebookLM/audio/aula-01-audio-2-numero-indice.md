Output language: English, conversational register. Keep the spoken output in English throughout, whatever language the uploaded sources are in.

Thesis. Asking whether an economy produced more this year than last is a question with no single correct answer, and the reason is not poor data. Adding up a basket whose composition changed requires prices as weights, the weights can be taken from either year, and the two choices give different answers that are both right. Everything the accounts do afterwards is an attempt to live with that.

Scope. Stay at the level of the uploaded lecture and of KURLAT section 1.2. If a source reaches past that level, note it in one sentence and move on.

Skip entirely: the three methods and the accounting identity, which the companion episode covers; welfare measures and inequality indices; and the institutional detail of how agencies collect prices. Do not attempt any multi-step calculation aloud.

SEGMENT ONE. THE QUESTION THAT SOUNDS SIMPLE. Open by setting up the comparison in the plainest terms: an economy makes two things, and between one year and the next it makes more of one and less of the other, while prices move too. Ask whether it produced more. Let one host insist the question obviously has an answer and that the job is just to strip out the price changes. Let the other ask which prices, and let the first host discover that the question has no obvious answer. That discovery is the whole hook, so end the segment there rather than resolving it.

SEGMENT TWO. THE FORK, OPENED. Take the same two years and value both baskets at the earlier year's prices, giving one growth rate. Then value both at the later year's prices, giving another. Name only those two growth rates aloud and no further arithmetic. Establish clearly that neither computation contains an error. Then give the two conventional names and attach each to its weights, so the listener leaves with the labels attached to the right thing rather than memorised in isolation.

SEGMENT THREE. WHY THE FORK EXISTS. This is the core and deserves the most room. Explain that weights are shares of spending, and that spending moves toward whatever has become relatively cheap. So a weight taken from the earlier year describes a basket people have since drifted away from, and a weight taken from the later year describes one they had not yet moved to. Make the direction concrete with a single example the listener can picture, something that collapsed in price and rose in quantity within living memory. Then have the hosts disagree about what follows: one holds that a best formula must exist and is simply hard to find, the other that the divergence is the honest signature of a question that was underdetermined from the start.

SEGMENT FOUR. THE STANDARD RESPONSE, AND ITS BILL. Introduce chaining as what statistical agencies actually do, described in words: compare each year only with the one beside it, using weights from that neighbourhood, then link the comparisons into a series. Give the gain, which is that no distant and arbitrary base year governs a comparison decades later. Then state the cost without hedging. The choice has not been removed, it is being made afresh every period. And the resulting series loses a property people rely on without noticing, because the pieces no longer add up to the whole. Have one host raise the objection that this feels like a bookkeeping inconvenience, and let the other explain why it changes how the numbers can be used.

SEGMENT FIVE. THE SAME PROBLEM, TWO DISGUISES. Close by showing that the listener has already met this problem twice more without being told. Comparing two countries at a moment in time is the same operation with the year replaced by a place, and the answer again depends on whose prices supply the weights, which is why converting at a market exchange rate and at purchasing power parity give different pictures of the same country. And the consumer price index that appears in the news is the identical machinery with a different basket. Give the listener one thing to carry away, phrased as a question to put to any real series they encounter: whose basket, and from which year.
