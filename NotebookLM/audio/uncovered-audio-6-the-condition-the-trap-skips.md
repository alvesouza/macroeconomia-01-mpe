Produce a two-host conversation about the New-Keynesian questions an exam can still ask after the one Prova 01 tested. Source: "Livro_Benigno_2015_NewKeynesianEconomics_AS_AD_View.pdf" (= BENIGNO: sections 6 and 7 at printed pages 511 to 514, section 8 at 514 to 516, section 9 at 516 to 518, section 11 at 521 to 523).

Thesis, and the conversation serves only this. Beyond the item Prova 01 tested, a BENIGNO question takes four shapes, each with a trap that skips one condition: after a temporary productivity gain output rises yet the gap is negative; after a mark-up shock the sign of the rate move depends on the mandate; after a spending rise the output multiplier is not the gap multiplier; at the zero bound the bank still has an instrument. The sentence that earns the mark states the skipped condition.

Five segments. Do not add a sixth.

SEGMENT ONE. A TEMPORARY PRODUCTIVITY GAIN. The question: show the shock and give the optimal policy. In BENIGNO, supply shifts down and demand does not move; prices fall and output rises, but by less than its natural level, because most prices are stuck, so the gap is negative. The trap: output rose, so hold or tighten. The sentence: cut the rate until prices are back at their expected level and the gap closes, with no trade-off, because the efficient level rose with the natural one. One sentence for BENIGNO's family of cases: a permanent gain needs no move, a merely expected gain needs a hike.

SEGMENT TWO. A MARK-UP SHOCK AND THE MANDATE. This is the segment the episode exists for; give it the most room. The question: oil, monopoly power or a payroll tax raises the mark-up for one period; what should the bank do? Natural output falls and efficient output does not, so stable prices and efficient output conflict. The best point is where the targeting line crosses the new supply curve; the line does not move, since efficient output did not. Whether the bank gets there by raising or cutting the rate depends on one comparison: is the targeting line flatter than demand? Its slope is set by the weight on prices, demand's by the intertemporal elasticity. Under the welfare weight, which BENIGNO finds well above that elasticity, the line is flatter, so the rate rises, by less than strict price stability would ask. A bank that mostly weighs output, with a line steeper than demand, cuts. The trap, seen on Prova 01, is carrying this comparison to a pure demand shock, where the target point does not move and the bank offsets the shift whatever its weight.

SEGMENT THREE. PUBLIC SPENDING AND THE RATE. The question: a temporary rise in public spending; what happens to output and the gap, and how should the bank respond? Natural and efficient output rise too, so in BENIGNO's table output rises by most of the spending and the gap by a small fraction of it. The trap is reporting the output multiplier as the success of the policy. The sentence: the gap opens positive, prices rise, private consumption is crowded out, and the bank should raise the rate to close the gap, crowding out consumption further. One clause of contrast: a short-run cut in sales and payroll taxes raises output and widens the gap.

SEGMENT FOUR. THE ZERO BOUND. The question: the natural real rate has turned negative and the nominal rate is at zero; what can policy do? The trap is that monetary policy is powerless and only fiscal policy works, which BENIGNO identifies as the old IS-LM answer. The sentence: the bank still sets the long-run price level, so a credible promise of higher future prices raises expected inflation, lowers the real rate at a zero nominal rate, and shifts demand up. On fiscal policy BENIGNO prefers measures that act on the future, such as lower future spending and taxes. One sentence only on deleveraging.

SEGMENT FIVE. THE REHEARSAL. Have the hosts write the four answers aloud, each in at most three sentences; after each, the other host names the condition the trap skipped. Close with the essay question in three sentences: the New-Keynesian model takes its method and rational expectations from the New Classicals, nominal rigidity from the Keynesians, and the interest rate as its instrument.

Skip entirely: the long-run mark-up news that Prova 01 tested, the derivation of either curve and of the loss, the multiplier table beyond one remark, and the paradoxes of deleveraging. Other episodes argue each shock at length; here each gets the question, the trap and the sentence. Stay at the level of BENIGNO; if it goes further, register it in one sentence and move on.

One instruction that belongs to audio and not to slides: before every answer, one host must say which curve moves and which gap the question is about, natural or efficient; the other names the condition a hurried answer would skip, and only then may the answer go on air. Never read an expression aloud; say what it decides.
