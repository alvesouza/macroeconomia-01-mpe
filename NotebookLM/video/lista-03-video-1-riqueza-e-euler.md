Build a narrated video overview. This is video 1 of 6 in a set organised by economic mechanism rather than by lecture, and it is built around a single visual object: the two-period consumption plane, with the budget line assembled from the two flow constraints and the optimum found by tangency. Every beat adds one element to that plane. Comparative statics belong to videos 2, 3 and 4; the frictions belong to videos 5 and 6.

Sources. Section 6.2 and exercise 6.1 of "Livro_Pablo_Kurlat_-_A_Course_in_Modern_Macroeconomics_2020_-_libgen.li.pdf" (= KURLAT), question 1 of "Lista_MPE_Macro1_2026_Lista3.pdf" (= LISTA3), and "Aula_MPE_Macro1_SlidesAula4.pdf" (= AULA4).

Output language: Brazilian Portuguese (pt-BR). On-screen labels in Portuguese, with the English term in parentheses where it is the standard one.

Thesis. The diagram is the model. A student who can rebuild this plane from an empty pair of axes can answer every item of the problem set by pointing at it; a student who only memorised the formula cannot say what any of its terms does.

Scope. Stay at the level of AULA4 and KURLAT section 6.2. Borrowing limits and taxes on returns are for later videos; if a source reaches into them, note it in one line and move on.

BEAT ONE. THE TWO CONSTRAINTS AS TWO POINTS. Open on empty axes with present consumption horizontal and future consumption vertical. Plot the endowment point, its coordinates being disposable income in each period. Draw two short arrows from it: one pointing down and right, labelled as saving turned into present consumption, and one up and left, labelled as saving carried forward. Narration states that every plan available to the household is reached from this one point by moving resources across time.

BEAT TWO. THE LINE APPEARS. Extend the two arrows into a full straight line through the endowment point. Put the slope on screen as the negative gross interest rate, and mark both intercepts. Narration states that the intercepts are the extreme plans, consume everything now or consume everything later, and that the slope is the price at which the market lets you trade between them. Transition: the endowment point stays lit while the line grows out of it in both directions, so it is clear the line is generated by the point and not the reverse.

BEAT THREE. THE SAME LINE, WRITTEN AS ONE EQUATION. Hold the line fixed and overlay the present-value budget constraint as text along it. Show the horizontal intercept being labelled with the lifetime-resources term, built on screen from three stacked pieces: initial wealth, income net of tax today, and income net of tax tomorrow discounted once. Narration states that these three pieces are interchangeable to the household, since only their sum determines which line it faces.

BEAT FOUR. PREFERENCES ENTER. Draw a family of indifference curves, convex to the origin, and let the highest attainable one settle into tangency with the line. Mark the tangency point and drop dashed guides to both axes for the two consumption levels. On screen, write the tangency condition as the marginal rate of substitution equal to the gross rate. Narration states that this equality is the Euler equation and nothing else.

BEAT FIVE. WHAT THE TANGENCY PINS DOWN. Keep the tangency point and draw a ray from the origin through it, labelling its slope as the ratio of future to present consumption. Now show three different budget lines, parallel to each other, each with its own tangency, and show all three tangency points falling on the same ray. Narration states that the ratio is fixed by patience, the interest rate and curvature alone, and that shifting resources moves the household along the ray without ever rotating it. Transition: the three lines fade and only the ray remains.

BEAT SIX. SAVING IS A DISTANCE. Return to one line, the endowment point and the tangency point together. Draw the horizontal gap between them and label it as saving. Show two contrasting cases side by side in one frame: a household whose tangency lies to the left of its endowment, which is lending, and one whose tangency lies to the right, which is borrowing. Put a small two-row table on screen giving the sign of the asset position in each case. Narration states that every later result in the problem set is a statement about how one of these two points moves.

Closing image. End on the single image the student should be able to redraw from memory: the axes, the endowment point, the line through it with its slope labelled, the tangency on the highest indifference curve, the ray from the origin carrying the consumption ratio, and the horizontal bracket marking saving. Hold the final frame while the narration states what the next video changes, which is a single one of the three pieces stacked in the intercept.
