Build a narrated video overview. This is video 3 of 6 in a set organised by economic mechanism rather than by lecture, and it is built around a single visual object: the derivative of present consumption with respect to the interest rate, plotted against the curvature parameter, crossing zero exactly once. Every beat works toward that crossing. The general rotation about an endowment point is treated elsewhere in the course; here the problem set removes future income, and this video follows that special case throughout.

Sources. Section 6.2 and exercise 6.3 of "Livro_Pablo_Kurlat_-_A_Course_in_Modern_Macroeconomics_2020_-_libgen.li.pdf" (= KURLAT), question 1 item c of "Lista_MPE_Macro1_2026_Lista3.pdf" (= LISTA3), and "Aula_MPE_Macro1_SlidesAula4.pdf" (= AULA4).

Thesis. One parameter decides the sign, and the problem set is asking for the condition rather than a direction. An answer that says saving rises, or that saving falls, is incomplete no matter which one it picks.

Scope. Stay at the level of AULA4 and KURLAT section 6.2. Credit limits and taxes on returns are for later videos; if a source reaches into them, note it in one line and move on.

BEAT ONE. THE PIVOT MOVES TO THE AXIS. Open on the two-period plane with the endowment point marked and future income being set to zero on screen, so the point slides down onto the horizontal axis. Draw the budget line through it. Narration states that with no income tomorrow the pivot of any rotation is this axis point, and that lifetime resources therefore do not change when the rate changes. Show the lifetime-resources label sitting fixed on the horizontal intercept.

BEAT TWO. THE ROTATION. Raise the interest rate and show the line pivoting about that axis point, becoming steeper, with the vertical intercept climbing while the horizontal intercept stays exactly where it is. Keep the old line faint on screen. Narration states that this is the only thing the interest rate does here, and that the household's resources were not touched.

BEAT THREE. THE FIRST FORCE. Draw a compensated line parallel to the new one and tangent to the original indifference curve, and show the optimum sliding along that curve away from present consumption. Mark this movement with a single arrow pointing left. Narration states that future consumption became cheaper and that this arrow always points the same way, for every household and every parameter value.

BEAT FOUR. THE SECOND FORCE, AND WHY IT HAS THE SIGN IT HAS. Move from the compensated line to the actual new line and mark the remaining movement with a second arrow pointing right. On screen, highlight the horizontal bracket between the axis pivot and the optimum, labelling it as a strictly positive asset position. Narration states that with no future income the household must be a lender, so a higher rate makes it richer and the second arrow points against the first. Transition: the two arrows are left alone on the diagram, head to head.

BEAT FIVE. THREE OPTIMA, THREE DIRECTIONS. Redraw the rotation once and overlay three optima on the original line, one for each of three curvature values, each with a coloured arrow to where it lands after the rate rises. Show the first arrow pointing left, the second straight up, and the third pointing right. Put a three-row table on screen with the curvature value, the elasticity of substitution and the resulting direction, and let the narration name only the middle row, where the arrow is vertical.

BEAT SIX. THE ANSWER AS A CURVE. Build a new chart with the curvature parameter on the horizontal axis and the derivative of present consumption on the vertical. Trace the curve rising from far below zero, crossing the axis, and flattening out above it. Shade the region left of the crossing and label it as substitution winning; shade the region right of it and label it as income winning. Mark the crossing point with a vertical guide. Narration states that this single crossing is what the problem set is asking for.

Closing image. End on one image the student should be able to redraw from memory: on the left, the budget line pivoting about the horizontal-axis point with the two opposing arrows; on the right, the derivative curve crossing zero once, with both regions shaded and the crossing marked. Hold the final frame while the narration states what the next video changes, which is a shift that moves the line without rotating it at all.
