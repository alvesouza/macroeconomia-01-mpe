Build a narrated video overview. This is video 3 of 6 in a set organised by economic mechanism rather than by question, and it is built around a single visual object: a dial for sigma that the viewer watches being turned, with every other quantity on screen responding to it. Every beat turns the dial once and shows what moved.

Sources. Section 9.1 of "Livro_Kurlat_Cap09_General_Equilibrium.pdf" (= KURLAT9), question 1 item e of "Lista_MPE_Macro1_2026_Lista5.pdf" (= LISTA5), and "Aula_Slides_Macro1_Aula6.pdf" (= AULA6).

Thesis. A productivity improvement raises the wage, and the wage pulls hours in two opposite directions at once. One parameter decides which pull wins, and it decides it visibly, as the sign of a numerator sitting above a denominator that can never turn negative.

Scope. Stay at the level of AULA6, KURLAT9 section 9.1 and the chapter 7 labour-supply material already covered. Videos 4 to 6 move to the capital margin; if a source reaches into them, note it in one line and move on.

Output language: English throughout, on-screen labels in English, whatever language the uploaded sources are in.

BEAT ONE. THE DIAL AND THE CROSSING. Open on the hours plot from the previous video, the rising curve against the flat line, with the crossing marked. Add a dial for sigma at the edge of the frame. Turn it from a low value to a high one and let the rising curve steepen and the crossing slide to the right. Narration states only that more curvature in utility means more hours worked in equilibrium, and that the rest of the video is about which way the crossing moves when productivity rises instead.

BEAT TWO. THE ELASTICITY ASSEMBLED FROM PARTS. Clear to a blank frame and build the elasticity expression piece by piece, the numerator first, then the three terms of the denominator arriving one at a time, each landing with a small plus sign beside it. Colour all three denominator terms the same, then colour the numerator differently. Narration states that all three denominator terms are strictly positive and therefore only the numerator can change sign.

BEAT THREE. THE SIGN FLIPPING. Show on screen a horizontal number line with zero marked, put the elasticity value on it as a sliding marker, and bind it to the sigma dial. Turn the dial slowly from below one to above one and let the marker slide across zero. Freeze exactly at the crossing and label that point. Narration states the answer LISTA5 asks for, that hours rise with productivity if and only if sigma is below one.

BEAT FOUR. THE TABLE THAT FILLS ITSELF. Show a five-row table on screen with columns for sigma, hours, the hours elasticity, the wage elasticity and the shared rental-rate and consumption elasticity. Fill it row by row as the dial turns: hours 0.144, 0.193, 0.265, 0.356, 0.451, and the hours elasticity running from plus 1.101 down to minus 0.795. Highlight the middle row, then light up the wage column and the last column to show both staying positive throughout. Narration names only the middle row and the fact that the last two columns never go negative.

BEAT FIVE. TWO ARROWS PULLING. Return to a diagram with consumption and leisure on the axes and rotate the budget line outward for a higher wage. Draw two arrows from the old optimum, one pointing toward more work labelled substitution, one pointing toward more leisure labelled income, and scale their lengths with the sigma dial. At the marked value they are exactly equal and opposite and the optimum does not move. Narration explains the arrows by how fast marginal utility falls, and nothing else.

BEAT SIX. THE SAME KNIFE EDGE, SEEN BEFORE. Split the frame. On the left keep the two arrows at the marked value. On the right show a short timeline of the course with the earlier labour-supply problem set flagged, where under the same preference the hours choice was independent of the wage and of the tax. Draw a line connecting the two panels. Narration states that logarithmic utility in consumption is the unique case where the two effects cancel, and that this is why growth models wanting stationary hours assume it.

Closing image. End on one image the student should be able to redraw from memory: the number line with the elasticity marker sitting exactly on zero, the sigma dial pointing at one, and the two opposing arrows below it drawn at identical length. Hold the final frame while the narration states what video 4 removes, which is the assumption that nothing can be carried from one period to the next.
