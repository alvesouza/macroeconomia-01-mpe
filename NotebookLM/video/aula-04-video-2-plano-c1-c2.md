Thesis. The whole apparatus of intertemporal consumption is one diagram: a line whose slope is set by the interest rate, a family of curves whose slope is set by impatience, and the point where they touch. Every result in the lecture is a movement of that line or a change in the curvature of those curves.

Scope. Stay at the level of AULA4 and KURLAT section 6.2. Borrowing constraints and taxes on returns belong to the fourth video; if a source reaches into them, note it in one line and move on.

BEAT ONE. THE ENDOWMENT POINT. Draw empty axes with first-period consumption horizontal and second-period consumption vertical. Mark a single point representing income in each period, and label it as what the household would consume if it neither saved nor borrowed. Narration states that this point is always available and is the anchor for everything that follows. The transition is the point beginning to glow as two arrows sprout from it, one heading down and right, the other up and left.

BEAT TWO. THE LINE THROUGH IT. Draw the budget line through the endowment point, extending in both directions. Show what each direction means with a small annotation: moving down and right converts future consumption into present consumption, which is borrowing; moving up and left is saving. Then mark the slope explicitly and show it being built from the interest rate. Narration states the price interpretation directly, that the slope is what one unit of consumption today costs in units of consumption tomorrow.

BEAT THREE. THE INTERCEPTS ARE THE CONSTRAINT. Extend the line to both axes and label each intercept: the horizontal one as the present value of lifetime resources, the vertical one as the same resources valued at the later date. Show the algebraic expression for the horizontal intercept appearing beside it. Narration draws the conclusion that reframes the whole problem, that the household is choosing a point on one line, so only the position of that line matters and not the composition of the income that produced it.

BEAT FOUR. PREFERENCES ARRIVE. Overlay a family of indifference curves, convex toward the origin, and show their slope at any point being read as the willingness to trade consumption between periods. Then let one curve rise until it just touches the budget line, and mark the point of tangency. Narration names the condition being satisfied there in words rather than symbols, that the rate at which the household is willing to substitute equals the rate at which the market lets it. Beside the diagram, display the Euler equation as the algebraic form of that same tangency.

BEAT FIVE. WHAT THE CURVATURE MEANS. Show two indifference maps side by side on the same budget line: one with gently curved indifference curves, one with sharply curved ones. Let the tangency point differ between them, with the sharply curved case sitting much closer to the diagonal where consumption is equal across periods. Narration names the parameter governing this and states its meaning, that it measures how strongly the household dislikes an uneven consumption path. Flag that the third video shows this same parameter deciding the sign of a key response.

BEAT SIX. THE ENDOWMENT MOVES, THE LINE DOES NOT ROTATE. Take the endowment point and slide it along the existing budget line, giving the household more income today and correspondingly less tomorrow. Show the line staying exactly where it is and the tangency point not moving at all, while the amount saved changes to absorb the difference. Narration lands the thesis: consumption depends on where the line is, not on where the household starts on it, which is why the timing of income, and later of taxes, drops out.

Closing image. End on one image the student should be able to redraw from memory: the budget line with the endowment point marked, both intercepts labelled, the tangent indifference curve, and the optimal point with saving shown as the horizontal distance from the endowment. Hold the final frame while the narration states the question the next video answers, which is what happens when the line rotates instead of the point sliding.
