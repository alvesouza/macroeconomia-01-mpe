Build a narrated video overview. This is video 2 of 4 for this lecture, and it is built around a single visual object: the fork that opens when the same quantity change is valued at two different sets of prices. Every beat develops that one object. The circular flow, purchasing power parity and welfare measures belong to the other three videos.

Sources. "Aula_MPE_Macro1_2026_Slides_1.pdf" (= AULA1) and chapter 1, section 1.2, of "Livro_Pablo_Kurlat_-_A_Course_in_Modern_Macroeconomics_2020_-_libgen.li.pdf" (= KURLAT).

Thesis. There is no neutral way to add up a changing basket of goods. The video shows the fork opening, shows why it opens, and shows that the standard fix relocates the choice rather than removing it.

Scope. Stay at the level of AULA1 and KURLAT section 1.2. If a source reaches past that level, note it in one line and move on.

BEAT ONE. TWO YEARS, ONE TABLE. Put a small table on screen with two goods across two years, showing price and quantity in each cell. Choose the numbers so one good becomes markedly cheaper while its quantity rises sharply. Narration reads only the direction of the two movements, not the cells. Then show nominal output for each year appearing as two bars beside the table. The transition is a question mark settling over the gap between the two bars, standing for the quantity that has to be separated out: how much of the change is prices and how much is goods.

BEAT TWO. THE FORK OPENS. Split the screen into two calculation panels that run in parallel. The left panel values both years at the earlier year's prices and ends in a growth rate. The right panel values both years at the later year's prices and ends in a different growth rate. Let the two panels compute line by line simultaneously so the divergence appears at the end rather than the beginning. Narration says those two growth rates aloud and no other figures, then states that both panels are arithmetically correct. The transition is the two panels sliding together under a single bracket labelled with the two index names.

BEAT THREE. WHY IT OPENS. Return to a single frame with quantity of one good on the horizontal axis and its relative price on the vertical. Plot the two years as two points and draw the movement between them. Beside the plot, show the expenditure share of the cheapening good as a bar that grows as its price falls. Narration draws the mechanism in words rather than numbers: weights are spending shares, spending shifts toward whatever became cheap, so a weight taken from the earlier year describes a basket that people have since abandoned. The transition is the bar continuing to grow while the earlier-year weight stays frozen beside it, so the mismatch is visible rather than argued.

BEAT FOUR. THE FIX AND ITS PRICE. Extend the table to a third year on screen. Show three short arrows, each linking consecutive years and each carrying its own weights, then show them compounding into one chained line. Place that line on the same chart as the two fixed-base lines from beat two. Narration states what has been gained, which is that no single arbitrary base year governs the whole series. Then it states the cost, pointing at a small panel where component values are added up and fail to match the chained total. The transition is that failure highlighting in a contrasting colour.

BEAT FIVE. THE SAME MACHINERY UNDER ANOTHER NAME. Show a two-row comparison on screen. The top row is the implicit deflator, defined as nominal over real, computed from the running example. The bottom row is a fixed-basket consumer price index over the same period. Let the two lines diverge on a shared chart. Beside them, show a three-item table naming the three sources of divergence: which basket, how often the weights update, and how imports enter. Narration points at each row as it is named.

BEAT SIX. THE GENERAL LESSON, DRAWN. Show a simple diagram with a single node labelled as the comparison to be made, and several outgoing edges, each labelled with an index formula and each ending in a different number. Narration lands the thesis: the branching is not a defect to be engineered away, because the question of which prices to use has no answer inside the data. It has an answer only once someone says what the comparison is for.

Closing image. End on one image the student should be able to redraw from memory: the two parallel calculation panels from beat two, side by side, ending in two different growth rates, with the substitution chart from beat three underneath as the explanation. Hold the final frame while the narration states the question that carries into the next video, which is what happens when the two baskets belong to two different countries rather than two different years.
