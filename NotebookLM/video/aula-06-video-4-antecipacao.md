Thesis. Consumption is a jump variable and capital is not. That single asymmetry is enough to make an economy respond today to an event scheduled for years from now, moving in a direction that looks wrong until you see which path it has to be standing on when the event arrives.

Scope. Stay at the level of AULA6 and KURLAT section 9.3. Assume the diagram from the third video is already built and on screen; do not re-derive the two loci. Do not attempt welfare comparisons or introduce uncertainty.

BEAT ONE. THE STARTING POINT, AND THE RULE. Open on the completed phase diagram with the economy resting at the intersection. State the two rules that do all the work, as a caption that stays on screen: capital cannot jump because it was inherited, and consumption must jump because the economy has to land on a converging path. Narration announces the experiment: at a future date, and not before, the production function is multiplied by a factor above one. Everyone learns this today.

BEAT TWO. WHERE THE ECONOMY IS HEADED. Show what the diagram looks like after the change, drawing the new loci in a second colour. The vertical line moves right, because a more productive technology sustains more capital at the same required return. The concave curve rises, because the same capital produces more. Mark the new intersection and the new saddle path. Narration is explicit that this is the destination, not the present.

BEAT THREE. THE JUMP THAT LOOKS LIKE A MISTAKE. Return to the present. Show consumption leaving the old steady-state level and jumping upward, immediately, on the announcement. Then show the arrow the old dynamics impose from that new point: with consumption above the old locus, saving falls and capital begins to decumulate. Narration confronts the paradox directly, that the economy answers good future news by getting poorer in capital, and asks the viewer to hold the objection one beat.

BEAT FOUR. THE OLD RULES GOVERN THE WAITING PERIOD. Trace the path from the jump point, under the old loci, as time runs forward toward the announced date. Show the trajectory drifting up and to the left, consumption rising while capital falls. Narration states why the old rules apply: the technology has not changed yet, so the equations that govern motion are still the old ones. Nothing about this arc is an equilibrium condition in itself; it is just the old dynamics carrying the economy somewhere.

BEAT FIVE. THE ARRIVAL CONDITION. Freeze the moment the change takes effect. Show the trajectory landing exactly on the new saddle path, and highlight that intersection point. Narration delivers the logic that fixes the whole path backwards: the initial jump was never free, it was pinned by the requirement that the drifting arc reach the new saddle path at exactly the announced date. Show one alternative jump, too large, drifting past it and diverging, to make the pinning visible.

BEAT SIX. THE REST IS ORDINARY. From the arrival point, trace the economy along the new saddle path to the new steady state, capital now accumulating and consumption rising with it. Show on screen, in a faded colour, what the exogenous-saving benchmark from the growth lectures predicts: nothing until the technology actually changes, then a smooth transition. Narration lands the thesis on that contrast, that endogenous saving lets news itself move the economy, and the first move runs opposite to the eventual one.

Closing image. End on one image the student should be able to redraw from memory: both pairs of loci, the initial jump drawn as a vertical arrow, the drifting arc under the old dynamics, the landing point on the new saddle path marked with the date, and the final approach to the new steady state. Hold the frame while the narration states the one sentence worth carrying, that the size of today's jump is decided entirely by a date in the future.
