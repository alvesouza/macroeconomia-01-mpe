Thesis. The model was written without any prices in it, and yet wages and interest rates can be recovered from it exactly, using one tangent line. That is not a trick: it is what competitive factor markets mean, and it is why the model can speak about income distribution without a separate theory of it.

Scope. Stay at the level of AULA3 and KURLAT sections 4.4 and 4.5. If a source reaches past that level, note it in one line and move on.

BEAT ONE. A MODEL WITH NO PRICES. Show the per-worker production function alone on screen, a single rising and flattening curve. Beside it, list what has been assumed so far as three short captions: a technology, a saving rate, a depreciation rate. Narration observes that nothing in that list is a price, and poses the question the video answers, which is where a wage could possibly come from. The transition is two empty labelled boxes appearing beside the chart, one for the wage and one for the return to capital.

BEAT TWO. THE TANGENT. Draw a tangent to the curve at the operating point. Extend it leftward until it meets the vertical axis. Highlight two features in turn: the slope of the tangent, and the height of its intercept. Narration names the slope as the extra output from one more unit of capital, and states that a competitive firm will hire capital until its rental rate equals that. The transition is the slope value flowing into the first of the two empty boxes.

BEAT THREE. WHAT IS LEFT OVER. Keep the tangent and now shade two regions on the chart: the rectangle under the tangent's slope, representing total payments to capital, and the remaining portion up to the curve, representing what is left for labour. Show that the leftover equals the tangent's vertical intercept. Narration explains the logic in words, that after capital is paid its marginal product, the remainder of output is what labour receives, and that this equals the marginal product of labour precisely because of constant returns to scale. The second box fills.

BEAT FOUR. THE SPLIT AS A BAR. Convert the two shaded regions into a single stacked bar, one block for capital and one for labour, with the proportions preserved. Then slide the operating point along the production function and let the bar's proportions respond. Under a Cobb-Douglas technology the proportions do not move at all, and the narration names that as the reason the model reproduces one of the constancies observed in the data. Show the capital block's share as a figure on screen and name the benchmark value the course uses.

BEAT FIVE. WHAT HAPPENS WHEN CAPITAL IS DESTROYED. Move the operating point sharply to the left, as after the shock studied in the previous lecture's exercises. Show the tangent becoming steeper and the intercept dropping. Narration reads both consequences off the picture: the return to capital rises and the wage falls, and it is the same movement producing both. Then let the point drift back to its original position and let the prices return with it, so the temporary nature of the effect is seen rather than asserted.

BEAT SIX. ADDING TECHNOLOGY WITHOUT BREAKING THE PICTURE. Relabel the horizontal axis from capital per worker to capital per efficiency unit, and show the curve staying exactly where it is while the units beneath it change. Then show two time plots beside it: output per efficiency unit, flat at the steady state, and output per worker, rising steadily. Narration states the result and then its uncomfortable side: living standards now grow at the assumed rate of technical progress, so growth is in the model because it was placed there.

Closing image. End on one image the student should be able to redraw from memory: the production function with its tangent, the intercept marked as the wage, the slope marked as the return to capital, and the stacked bar beside it showing the resulting split of output. Hold the final frame while the narration states the question the next video asks, which is whether this calibrated machinery can account for how far apart rich and poor countries actually are.
