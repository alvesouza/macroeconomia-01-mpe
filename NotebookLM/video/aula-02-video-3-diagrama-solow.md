Thesis. The whole model is one picture with three curves, and every conclusion in the lecture is read off it. The video builds the picture in the order the assumptions arrive, so that each curve is understood as the consequence of something assumed rather than as a shape to memorise.

Scope. Stay at the level of AULA2 and KURLAT sections 4.1 and 4.2. The Golden Rule, factor markets and technological progress belong to the next lecture; if a source reaches into them, note it in one line and move on.

BEAT ONE. THE AXES, AND WHY THEY ARE PER WORKER. Draw an empty chart with capital per worker on the horizontal axis. Before any curve appears, show the reduction that justifies these axes: an aggregate production function on screen, then both sides divided by the labour force, collapsing two arguments into one. Narration states what constant returns to scale buys, which is that the size of the economy drops out and only the ratio matters. The transition is the aggregate expression fading and the per-worker one remaining above the empty axes.

BEAT TWO. THE FIRST CURVE. Draw output per worker as a curve rising from the origin and flattening. Mark two features explicitly on the drawing: the slope at the origin, drawn steep enough to be near vertical, and the slope far to the right, drawn approaching flat. Narration names the Inada conditions as the source of those two features and states what each will be used for later. Then shade the vertical distance from the curve down to the horizontal axis and label it as output available to be split.

BEAT THREE. THE SECOND CURVE. Show the output curve being scaled down by a constant fraction to produce the saving curve, drawn beneath it with the same shape. Keep the gap between the two curves visible and label it as consumption per worker. Narration states plainly that the saving rate is being assumed rather than derived, and flags that a later lecture will replace this assumption with a household's choice. The transition is the saving fraction being dragged up and down while the curve rescales, so the viewer sees it as a dial rather than a fact.

BEAT FOUR. THE THIRD CURVE, AND THE CROSSING. Draw effective depreciation as a straight line through the origin, and build its slope on screen from its two parts, the depreciation rate and the population growth rate, so the viewer sees why it is a sum. Let it intersect the saving curve. Mark the crossing and label it the steady state. Narration explains why exactly one crossing exists, using the two slope features established in beat two: one curve starts above and ends below, and its curvature prevents a second meeting.

BEAT FIVE. WHY THE ECONOMY GOES THERE. Place a marker on the horizontal axis to the left of the crossing and show the vertical gap between the saving curve and the depreciation line, shading it as net accumulation. Animate the marker moving right, with the shaded gap narrowing as it approaches the crossing. Then repeat from the right-hand side, where the gap has the opposite sign and the marker moves left. Narration states the stability result and points at the shrinking gap as its proof. Beside the diagram, plot the corresponding path of capital per worker against time for both starting points.

BEAT SIX. THE TWO DIALS. With the finished diagram on screen, move the population growth rate and let the depreciation line rotate, showing the crossing slide. Then move the saving rate and let the saving curve shift, showing the crossing slide the other way. Narration names which curve each parameter moves, and flags the error the exercises punish: population growth acts on the straight line, not on the saving curve. Show both shifts once more in quick succession so the pairing is fixed.

Closing image. End on one image the student should be able to redraw from memory: the complete diagram with all three curves, the crossing marked, the consumption gap shaded, and the two arrows on the horizontal axis showing motion toward the crossing from either side. Hold the final frame while the narration states the question the last video answers, which is what happens to growth, and not merely to the level, when one of those dials is moved.
