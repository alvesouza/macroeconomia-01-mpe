Build a narrated video overview. This is video 4 of 6 in a set organised by economic mechanism rather than by question, and it is built around a single visual object: one equation held fixed in the centre of the frame while the world behind it is swapped, so that the same symbols come to mean something different. Every beat swaps one piece of the background.

Sources. Sections 9.1 and 9.3 of "Livro_Kurlat_Cap09_General_Equilibrium.pdf" (= KURLAT9), question 1 item d and question 2 item a of "Lista_MPE_Macro1_2026_Lista5.pdf" (= LISTA5), and "Aula_Slides_Macro1_Aula6.pdf" (= AULA6).

Thesis. The Euler equation is identical in both halves of the problem set, but in one of them the consumption path is already fixed by production, so the interest rate has nothing to allocate and merely reports impatience, while in the other the same equation becomes a constraint the technology has to satisfy.

Scope. Stay at the level of AULA6 and KURLAT9. Videos 5 and 6 take the no-arbitrage condition and the steady state; if a source reaches into them, note it in one line and move on.

Output language: English throughout, on-screen labels in English, whatever language the uploaded sources are in.

BEAT ONE. THE EQUATION THAT WILL NOT MOVE. Open with the Euler equation alone in the centre of an empty frame, and pin it there with a visible marker so the viewer sees it stays put for the rest of the video. Around it, animate the perturbation: a small block of consumption lifted out of today's bar and dropped into tomorrow's, with the discount factor shown shrinking it on arrival. Narration states that the equation is a no-arbitrage condition on the household's own plan, cost today against discounted benefit tomorrow.

BEAT TWO. THE FIRST BACKGROUND. Fill the space behind the equation with two output bars, one per period, and lock consumption to them with rigid connectors, so neither bar can change height. Then show the interest rate expression assembling from the ratio of the two bars. Narration states that with no storage the quantities were settled before the price appeared.

BEAT THREE. THE EMPTY SLOT. Keep the frame and slide a labelled tile reading marginal product of capital toward the interest rate expression. Show it bouncing off and coming to rest outside the box, with the space inside visibly unfilled. Then level the two output bars and let the expression collapse to a single term, one over beta, displayed large. Narration gives one number, the rate implied by a discount factor of 0.96, and says that the rate here reports rather than allocates.

BEAT FOUR. THE SECOND BACKGROUND. Clear the bars and replace them with an unbounded timeline of periods running off the right edge, with an asset line threading through them. At the far end, show a debt balance growing without limit and then a barrier dropping in front of it labelled with the borrowing limit condition. Narration states what the condition rules out and what would break without it, namely that the problem would have no solution at all.

BEAT FIVE. THE PATH TILTING. Plot consumption against time as a straight path and put the interest rate on a slider beside a fixed mark for impatience. Drag the slider above the mark and the path tilts upward; drag it below and the path tilts down; rest it on the mark and the path goes flat. Then add a second slider for sigma and show it changing the steepness of the tilt without ever moving the flat case. Narration states the growth condition once and then only that one slider changes speed while the other changes direction.

BEAT SIX. THE TWO WORLDS ON ONE TABLE. Split the frame into two columns headed frozen capital and accumulable capital, with four rows: what fixes the consumption path, what fixes the interest rate, whether the marginal product of capital appears, and what a productivity rise does. Fill the cells and then light up the single row where the two columns disagree. Narration names only that row.

Closing image. End on one image the student should be able to redraw from memory: the Euler equation pinned in the centre, with the two-period output bars behind it on one side and the unbounded timeline behind it on the other, and beneath the whole frame the sentence that preferences determine the interest rate while technology determines the quantity of capital. Hold the final frame while the narration states that video 5 proves the second half of that sentence.
