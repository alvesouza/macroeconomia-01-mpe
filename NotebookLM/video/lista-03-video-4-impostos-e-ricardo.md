Build a narrated video overview. This is video 4 of 6 in a set organised by economic mechanism rather than by lecture, and it is built around a single visual object: the endowment point sliding along a budget line that does not move, while the tangency stays exactly where it was. Every beat develops that immobility. The credit limit is video 5 and the tax on returns is video 6.

Sources. Section 6.2 and exercise 6.1 of "Livro_Pablo_Kurlat_-_A_Course_in_Modern_Macroeconomics_2020_-_libgen.li.pdf" (= KURLAT), with attention to the caveat page in that section, question 1 items d and e of "Lista_MPE_Macro1_2026_Lista3.pdf" (= LISTA3), and "Aula_MPE_Macro1_SlidesAula4.pdf" (= AULA4).

Thesis. A lump-sum tax cannot distort anything, because there is no behaviour that would reduce it. Once that is seen on the diagram, the equivalence result is not a surprise but a consequence, and the whole adjustment shows up in one line segment.

Scope. Stay at the level of AULA4 and KURLAT section 6.2. Credit limits and taxes on saving returns are later videos; if a source reaches into them, note it in one line and move on.

BEAT ONE. A TAX THAT ONLY SHIFTS. Open on the two-period plane with the endowment point, the budget line and the tangency. Levy a tax today and show the endowment point moving straight left, then the line being redrawn through it with the identical slope, shifting inward in parallel. Keep the old line faint on screen. Narration states that the slope did not change because no price changed.

BEAT TWO. THE OPTIMUM MOVES DOWN A FIXED RAY. Draw the ray from the origin through the original tangency and show the new tangency landing on that same ray, closer to the origin. Drop dashed guides from both tangencies to each axis. Put a two-row table on screen for present and future consumption, with a down arrow in each. Narration states that both consumptions fall in the same proportion, and that the ratio between them never moved.

BEAT THREE. WHY CONSUMPTION FALLS BY LESS THAN THE TAX. Overlay two horizontal brackets on the same diagram: the leftward shift of the endowment, which is the size of the tax, and the smaller leftward shift of the tangency, which is the fall in present consumption. Show the difference between the two brackets highlighted separately and labelled as the fall in saving. Narration states that the two pieces add up to the tax exactly, and that this is smoothing made visible.

BEAT FOUR. A TAX TOMORROW PUSHES SAVING THE OTHER WAY. Reset the diagram and levy the tax in the second period instead, so the endowment point moves straight down. Show the line again shifting inward in parallel and the tangency again sliding down the same ray. Now show the bracket between endowment and tangency growing rather than shrinking. Put a small table on screen with two columns, tax today and tax tomorrow, and three rows, and highlight the single cell where the sign differs. Narration names only that cell.

BEAT FIVE. THE SWAP. Return to the general case and run the experiment: cut the tax today and raise the tax tomorrow by the gross rate. Animate the endowment point sliding down and to the right, and show it landing back on the very same budget line rather than on a new one. Hold the line and the tangency perfectly still throughout the animation. Narration states that the present value of taxes did not change, so the feasible set did not change, so the choice did not change. Transition: everything dims except the endowment point and the tangency, with the bracket between them visibly longer than before.

BEAT SIX. WHAT ACTUALLY CHANGED, AND WHAT THE RESULT DOES NOT CLAIM. Split the frame. On the left, a two-row bar chart of public and private saving, one bar falling and the other rising by the same amount, with a third bar for their sum staying flat. On the right, a four-row table of the assumptions behind the result, with the first two rows highlighted as the ones the next two videos will break. Narration states the common misreading and corrects it: the level and composition of government spending still matter, and so does the type of tax.

Closing image. End on one image the student should be able to redraw from memory: a single budget line, two endowment points on it connected by an arrow, one unmoved tangency, and the two horizontal brackets of different length showing that saving absorbed the entire adjustment. Hold the final frame while the narration states what the next video removes, which is the household's freedom to slide along that line.
