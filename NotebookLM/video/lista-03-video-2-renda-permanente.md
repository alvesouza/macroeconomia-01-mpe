Build a narrated video overview. This is video 2 of 6 in a set organised by economic mechanism rather than by lecture, and it is built around a single visual object: the budget line shifting outward in parallel when only future income is revised upward, and the tangency point sliding along a fixed ray from the origin. Every beat develops that parallel shift. The rotation caused by the interest rate belongs to video 3; taxes to video 4.

Sources. Sections 6.1 and 6.2 and exercise 6.4 of "Livro_Pablo_Kurlat_-_A_Course_in_Modern_Macroeconomics_2020_-_libgen.li.pdf" (= KURLAT), question 1 item b of "Lista_MPE_Macro1_2026_Lista3.pdf" (= LISTA3), and "Aula_MPE_Macro1_SlidesAula4.pdf" (= AULA4).

Output language: Brazilian Portuguese (pt-BR). On-screen labels in Portuguese, with the English term in parentheses where it is the standard one.

Thesis. Consumption tracks lifetime resources, not the pay slip of the current period. The clean way to see it is to change something that lies entirely in the future and watch today's decision move anyway.

Scope. Stay at the level of AULA4 and KURLAT sections 6.1 and 6.2. Credit limits and distortionary taxes are for later videos; if a source reaches into them, note it in one line and move on.

BEAT ONE. THE KEYNESIAN PREDICTION, DRAWN TO BE REFUTED. Open on a different pair of axes: current income horizontal, current consumption vertical, with the Keynesian line rising from a positive intercept with slope less than one. Place a household on it. Now write on screen that expected future income has just risen, and show the point not moving at all, because the horizontal coordinate did not change. Narration states that this immobility is the prediction under test.

BEAT TWO. SWITCH PLANES. Cut to the two-period consumption plane from video 1, with the endowment point, the budget line through it, and the tangency on the highest indifference curve. Label the three stacked pieces of the horizontal intercept: initial wealth, income today net of tax, and income tomorrow net of tax discounted once. Narration states that the future piece is about to grow and nothing else will be touched.

BEAT THREE. THE ENDOWMENT RISES VERTICALLY. Animate the endowment point moving straight up, since only the second coordinate changed. Show the budget line being redrawn through the new endowment with the same slope, so the whole line shifts outward in parallel. Keep the old line faint on screen. Narration states that the slope is untouched because no price changed, only resources.

BEAT FOUR. THE OPTIMUM SLIDES ALONG A RAY. Draw the ray from the origin through the original tangency and show the new tangency landing on the same ray, further out. Drop dashed guides from both tangencies to the horizontal axis and highlight the gap between them. Narration states that present consumption rose even though present income did not, and that the ratio of the two consumptions never moved. Transition: the two tangencies stay lit while the rest of the diagram dims, so only the movement along the ray remains.

BEAT FIVE. SAVING GOES THE OTHER WAY. Bring back both endowment points and both tangencies. Draw the horizontal bracket between endowment and tangency in each case and show the second bracket being shorter. Put a small table on screen with two columns, before and after, and three rows: present consumption, future consumption, saving, filling in the direction of each. Narration names only the row where the sign differs from the others.

BEAT SIX. TRANSITORY AGAINST PERMANENT. Split the frame into two panels showing the same starting point. In the left panel, raise income today alone; in the right, raise income in both periods by the same amount. Show that the left panel's line shifts out by less and its tangency barely moves horizontally, while the right panel's line shifts out by more and its tangency moves a lot. Put the two propensities on screen as a two-row table and let the narration name only the contrast between them, not the numbers themselves.

Closing image. End on one image the student should be able to redraw from memory: the two parallel budget lines, the fixed ray from the origin carrying both tangencies, and the two horizontal brackets showing saving shrinking as consumption grows. Hold the final frame while the narration states what the next video changes, which is the slope of the line rather than its position.
