Build a narrated video overview. This is video 6 of 6 in a set organised by economic mechanism rather than by question, and it is built around a single visual object: the consumption hump, with two points marked on it that never coincide. Every beat either builds the hump or moves one of the points along it. This video closes the set.

Sources. Section 9.3, equation 9.3.17 and exercises 9.11 and 9.12 of "Livro_Kurlat_Cap09_General_Equilibrium.pdf" (= KURLAT9), question 2 items d and e of "Lista_MPE_Macro1_2026_Lista5.pdf" (= LISTA5), and "Aula_Slides_Macro1_Aula6.pdf" (= AULA6).

Thesis. The economy comes to rest to the left of the peak of its own consumption hump, and the distance is exactly impatience. It is not a failure, it closes as patience rises, and it costs far less consumption than the size of the gap suggests, because a hump is flat at the top.

Scope. Stay at the level of AULA6 and KURLAT9 section 9.3, treating the phase diagram graphically as AULA6 treats it. Earlier videos supplied the arbitrage condition and the Euler equation.

Output language: English throughout, on-screen labels in English, whatever language the uploaded sources are in.

BEAT ONE. BUILDING THE HUMP. Open on axes with capital horizontal and consumption vertical. Draw the output curve rising and bending over, then draw the straight replacement-investment line beneath it, then subtract one from the other so the concave hump emerges and settles. Mark its peak with a hollow dot. Narration states that the hump is output minus what depreciation eats, and that in a steady state all investment is replacement.

BEAT TWO. FOUR SUBSTITUTIONS, ONE VERTICAL LINE. Show four short equations arriving in sequence at the left of the frame, each one feeding the next: constant consumption, then arbitrage, then the firm's condition, then the result. As the fourth lands, drop a vertical line onto the hump at the capital value it names, and mark where it meets the curve with a solid dot. Tag each of the four arrivals as preferences or technology as it appears. Narration names only which tag sits on the first arrival.

BEAT THREE. THE LINE THAT WILL NOT MOVE. Add a slider for productivity and turn it up. Show the hump rising and widening while the vertical line stays exactly where it was, and a readout for the interest rate holding steady beside it. Then turn a second slider for the discount factor and show the vertical line finally sliding. Narration states the sentence the whole video is for: preferences determine the interest rate, technology determines the capital stock.

BEAT FOUR. THE PHASE DIAGRAM BEHIND THE HUMP. Switch to the phase plane. Draw the vertical locus for constant consumption, then the concave locus for constant capital, then their intersection, then the four regions with direction arrows, then the single trajectory that converges. Alongside, show a second panel with capital moving smoothly through time while consumption jumps at the first instant. Narration states why the first locus is vertical, namely that consumption does not enter its condition at all, and separately that capital cannot jump while consumption must.

BEAT FIVE. THE TWO POINTS, AND THE GAP. Return to the hump with both dots on it, the hollow one at the peak and the solid one to its left. Put the two conditions on screen as consecutive lines with the extra term boxed in the lower one. Then run the discount factor slider toward one and let the solid dot crawl toward the hollow one and meet it. Show a table of four rows for discount factors 0.900, 0.960, 0.990 and 0.999 with the capital values 2.537, 5.082, 8.066 and 9.502 beside them. Narration names only that the boxed term is the whole distance.

BEAT SIX. THE GAP THAT COSTS ALMOST NOTHING. Keep both dots and drop two brackets onto the frame: a long horizontal bracket between them on the capital axis, and a very short vertical bracket between their heights on the consumption axis. Zoom into the peak so the curve there looks almost flat. Put two readouts on screen, a capital shortfall of 1.9 per cent against a consumption shortfall of 0.006 per cent. Narration states that a maximum has zero slope, so the loss from being off it is second order, and that this is why claims about large losses from under-accumulating are usually weak.

Closing image. End on one image the student should be able to redraw from memory: the consumption hump with a hollow dot at the peak and a solid dot to its left, a long horizontal bracket between them and a barely visible vertical one, and the single boxed term written underneath as the name of the distance. Hold the final frame while the narration states that this shortfall is the optimum and that the Golden Rule is a benchmark rather than a target.
